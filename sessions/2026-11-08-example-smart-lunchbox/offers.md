# Round 2: offer or out

Cards saved verbatim as each Shark returned them. Result: all five Sharks out, so negotiation is skipped (all-out override).

## Mr. Wonderful

SHARK: MR. WONDERFUL
WHAT THE ANSWERS CHANGED: The landed cost answer moved me further away. At 36 landed, 25 in ads, and about 3 a year for the app, a direct sale nets roughly 5 and a retail unit nets about 2.
The inventory answer helped, a little. But a royalty can only repay me from a margin, and there is barely one here.

DECISION: OUT

IF OUT, WHY: You want 2.5 million for about 140,000 in sales. That is 18 times revenue on a lunchbox people buy once a year, and each unit nets you single digits. I can't get my soldiers home from 5 dollars a box, and if I took even a 3 dollar royalty you would have almost nothing left to grow on. You are dead to me. I'm out. (Valuation reality check, Margin first)

WHAT WOULD HAVE GOT ME IN: Net of 15 dollars or more per unit, all in. Get landed cost under 25 or charge a subscription for the app. Then I would offer the 250,000 for a per-unit royalty until I am repaid, plus low equity.

INVESTABILITY: 2/10
PILLAR SCORE: 2/10
CONFIDENCE: 4/5
RISK: likely. Every retail unit runs on about 2 dollars of margin. The day returns or a freight spike show up, you lose money on each box you sell, right in the middle of the back-to-school order you just borrowed to fill.

ONE LINE FOR THE ENTREPRENEUR: Stop. What is your net margin? Not gross. Net. Until it is double digits per box, this is a hobby, not a business.

## Lori

IF OUT, WHY: I love the problem, and I could demo a full box next to an empty one in seconds. But the shelf math is broken (Margin to shelf). At 69 dollars it isn't an impulse yes, and at 41 wholesale nobody in the chain makes money, including you. So I'm out.
WHAT WOULD HAVE GOT ME IN: A quote from your factory showing landed cost at 18 or less at 10,000 units. Then I could put this on QVC at about 49 and give the retailer, the network, and you each a real margin.

INVESTABILITY: 3/10
PILLAR SCORE: 4/10
CONFIDENCE: 4/5
RISK: likely   (a big lunchbox brand adds a cheap sensor, and with a 24 percent wholesale margin every new retailer burns cash)

ONE LINE FOR THE ENTREPRENEUR: A hero on TV still dies on the shelf if the price can't pay everyone, so cut your cost before you chase a single new store.

## Cuban

SHARK: CUBAN
WHAT THE ANSWERS CHANGED: Nothing moved me toward in. The sensor answer pushed me further out: a few dollars a year to run it, nobody tracks it, no subscription, and nobody opens the app after the first month.
The landed cost answer (5 to 10 dollars a unit at wholesale) means even a brand play doesn't work, so I can't fall back on my own rule that brands win without tech.

DECISION: OUT
IF OUT, WHY: You're asking 2.5 million on about 140k in sales, roughly 18x, and the one edge you have is a sensor Bentgo can put in its box next fall for 5 dollars. A patent on a latch isn't a moat (Er-and-est test, Competitor's eye), and you don't know what the app costs you per unit, so you don't know your margin. For those reasons, I'm out.
WHAT WOULD HAVE GOT ME IN: The all-in yearly cost of running the sensor and app per unit, plus proof that parents keep using it after September. If they did, that would be a paid subscription and a data story a big brand can't copy overnight.

INVESTABILITY: 2/10
PILLAR SCORE: 2/10
CONFIDENCE: 4/5
RISK: likely (one big lunchbox brand adds a cheap sensor, and a company with thin wholesale margin and no trademark has nothing left to stand on)
ONE LINE FOR THE ENTREPRENEUR: Look at yourself the way your biggest rival would, then build the thing they can't copy in one season, and know what every unit costs you before you ask anyone for money. And if someone at this table offers you a royalty, do the math on what it does to your cash in a slow month before you shake.

INVESTABILITY: 3/10

ONE LINE FOR THE ENTREPRENEUR: You can sell, so stop selling to me and go get your partner all the way in. A half-committed team is half a bet, and nobody buys half a bet.
