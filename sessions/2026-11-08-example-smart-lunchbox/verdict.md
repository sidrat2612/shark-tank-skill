# Verdict: Smart lunchbox

Session: sessions/2026-11-08-example-smart-lunchbox/ | Mode: full + no-questions | Panel: Mr. Wonderful, Lori, Cuban, Barbara, Daymond

## 1. The pitch

An insulated school lunchbox with an app-connected sensor that reports food temperature and whether the child ate. Sold 2,400 units in 11 months, mostly direct, plus two regional retailers.

**The ask:** 250,000 for 10%. Implied valuation 250,000 / 0.10 = 2,500,000.

## 2. The verdict

**No deal**

Every Shark went out, and the panel score is 2.5, below the 4.0 line for a come-back-when label. The room agreed on one thing: a 69 lunchbox that costs 31 or more to land leaves 5 to 10 a unit at wholesale, and nothing in the pitch protects that thin margin from a bigger brand.

## 4. Why the outs went out

- **Mr. Wonderful:** You want 2.5 million for about 140,000 in sales. That is 18 times revenue on a lunchbox people buy once a year, and each unit nets you single digits. I can't get my soldiers home from 5 dollars a box, and if I took even a 3 dollar royalty you would have almost nothing left to grow on. You are dead to me. I'm out. (Valuation reality check, Margin first)
- **Lori:** I love the problem, and I could demo a full box next to an empty one in seconds. But the shelf math is broken (Margin to shelf). At 69 dollars it isn't an impulse yes, and at 41 wholesale nobody in the chain makes money, including you. So I'm out.
- **Cuban:** You're asking 2.5 million on about 140k in sales, roughly 18x, and the one edge you have is a sensor Bentgo can put in its box next fall for 5 dollars. A patent on a latch isn't a moat (Er-and-est test, Competitor's eye), and you don't know what the app costs you per unit, so you don't know your margin. For those reasons, I'm out.
- **Barbara:** I like the full-time founder. Anybody who sells 2,400 lunchboxes to parent groups has grit. But half a team won't commit and nobody owns the money, so I'd be betting on one person carrying two jobs at 18 times sales. I'm out.
- **Daymond:** I'm out. At 41 wholesale on a 36 landed cost, every retailer you open loses you money. With no trademark and one factory for each part, I have no brand to license and no supply chain I can trust for back to school.

## 6. The valuation check

- Ask: 2,500,000. At most 165,600 in 11-month revenue if every unit sold at retail: **15.1x**. On the roughly 140,000 blended revenue the Sharks assumed after retailer units: **17.9x**.
- Offers: none, so there is no median offer valuation.
- The Sharks priced it at zero because the per-unit margin, not the multiple, broke the deal. Mr. Wonderful, Barbara, and Daymond each named a path back: a net of about 15 a box, or landed cost under 25.

## 7. The question that hurt the most

**Lori, Daymond, and Mr. Wonderful asked:** What does it really cost to land one unit, all in?
**Answer used (assumed, no-questions run):** the 31 is a factory price; landed is 31 to 36 against a 41 wholesale price, leaving 5 to 10 a unit.
Every Shark cited this answer in Round 2, and every Shark's investability fell between Round 1 and Round 3 (Barbara the most, 5 to 3).

## 8. Three things to fix before pitching real investors

1. **Get landed cost under 25, ideally 18, and prove it.** Bring a signed quote, sensor included, from a factory that has run a 10,000 unit order. (Lori, Daymond, Mr. Wonderful)
2. **Know the sensor and app numbers.** Measure the yearly running cost per unit (target under 3) and weekly use after the first month. If parents keep using it, test a paid subscription. (Cuban, Mr. Wonderful)
3. **Commit the team and protect the name.** Put the part-time co-founder on a full-time date within 90 days, give one founder the books, file a trademark, and widen the patent past the latch. (Barbara, Daymond, Lori)
