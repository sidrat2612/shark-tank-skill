# Round 1: the grilling (no-questions mode)

Cards saved verbatim as each Shark returned them.

## Mr. Wonderful

SHARK: MR. WONDERFUL (Money, valuation, and return on capital)
FIRST IMPRESSION: You want 250,000 for 10 percent. That is 2.5 million on at most 165,600 in sales, so 15 times revenue on a lunchbox. That is crazy.

ASSUMPTIONS (ranked by how much they change your decision):
1. I would ask: What were real sales after the retailer units, and what is your net margin? I will assume: about 140,000 in blended revenue and net close to breakeven. That makes it roughly 18 times sales. Cuts hard against them (Valuation reality check).
2. I would ask: What does the app and sensor cost per unit per year, and do you charge a subscription? I will assume: running costs of a few dollars per unit and no subscription. So the app is a cost, not recurring revenue. Cuts against (Recurring revenue premium).
3. I would ask: What does a customer cost you on the website? I will assume: 20 to 30 dollars in paid ads. That leaves 38 dollars gross down to single digits. Cuts against (Margin first).
4. I would ask: What will my 250,000 do? I will assume: mostly inventory for back to school. Good. Inventory turns into cash inside one season, so a royalty can repay me (Royalty payback rule).

LEANING: need answers
PROVISIONAL INVESTABILITY: 3/10
PROVISIONAL PILLAR SCORE: 2/10   (Valuation and capital return)
DEAL BREAKER IF: Net margin per direct sale is negative once ad spend and app costs come out. Then every unit sold burns my soldiers, and I am out.

## Lori

SHARK: LORI (Consumer product, retail, and TV/QVC sellability)
FIRST IMPRESSION: Every parent knows this worry, and on TV I could show a full lunchbox next to an empty one in seconds. But a 69 dollar lunchbox with an app is a lot to ask, and at 31 to make I worry the shelf math is broken before it starts.

ASSUMPTIONS (ranked by how much they change your decision):
1. I would ask: What is your landed cost and your real wholesale price? I will assume: landed is about 31, and wholesale is 41 with no room to raise it. (Margin to shelf) Retail at 2.2 times cost leaves about 10 dollars per unit at wholesale. QVC and big-box both need more than that. This cuts hard against.
2. I would ask: What does the provisional patent cover, and can someone build around it? I will assume: it covers the latch only, and a knockoff could use a different latch with the same sensor. (Protection check) Weak protection on a product that is easy to copy. Cuts against.
3. I would ask: Can you get the price under 50 and fill 10,000 units before back to school? I will assume: no cost-down plan yet, and the contract manufacturer has never run an order that size. (Impulse price point, Can you fill the order) Cuts against, but I can fix both if the factory cooperates.

LEANING: need answers
PROVISIONAL INVESTABILITY: 4/10
PROVISIONAL PILLAR SCORE: 6/10
DEAL BREAKER IF: landed cost cannot get below about 18 dollars. Without that, no retail price leaves the retailer, QVC, and the company each a real margin.

## Cuban

SHARK: CUBAN (Tech, scale, and competitive moat)
FIRST IMPRESSION: 2,400 units in 11 months is real. I respect that. But 2.5 million on maybe 140k in revenue is about 18x, and right now that sensor looks like a feature, not a company.

ASSUMPTIONS (ranked by how much they change your decision):
1. I would ask: If Bentgo or a big lunchbox brand put a 5 dollar sensor in their box next fall, what stops them? I will assume: Nothing but a provisional patent on a latch, which they design around. That cuts out. (Competitor's eye, Er-and-est test)
2. I would ask: What does the app and sensor cost you per user per year, and does anyone open it after September? I will assume: Ongoing cost is not tracked, and app use falls off after the first month. That cuts out. It means no data moat and no recurring revenue, just a 69 dollar box bought once a year.
3. I would ask: What were sales last month, all in, after retailer margin? I will assume: Blended revenue near 140k, mostly direct, with CAC unknown. That cuts against. At 24% wholesale margin, retail growth burns cash. (Know your numbers drill)

LEANING: out
PROVISIONAL INVESTABILITY: 3/10
PROVISIONAL PILLAR SCORE: 2/10 (Defensibility and scale)
DEAL BREAKER IF: The founder can't give me the all-in cost of running the sensor and app per unit. If they don't know that, they don't know their margin.

## Barbara

SHARK: BARBARA (The founder, sales grit, and gut read)
FIRST IMPRESSION: 2,400 lunchboxes in 11 months, mostly off their own website, tells me somebody here can sell. My mother packed ten lunches a day and never once wondered if we ate them, so I need to see that this problem is real, and I need to know why one of the two founders is only half here.

ASSUMPTIONS (ranked by how much they change your decision):
1. I would ask: Who is part-time, and when do they go full-time? I will assume: the part-time one keeps a day job until funding lands and won't commit to a date. This cuts against them. Half a team is half a bet (Buy the entrepreneur).
2. I would ask: Who sold the first hundred units, and who runs the money? I will assume: the full-time founder sold them through friends and parent groups, and nobody really owns the money. Sales grit is a plus. No container is a minus (Expanders and containers).
3. I would ask: What was the worst moment, and what did you do next? I will assume: a sensor or app bug caused returns, they fixed it, and they kept going. That's a mild plus, but with no CAC, no sensor running costs and no projection, I can't plan a payback (Rebound test, Payback first).

LEANING: need answers
PROVISIONAL INVESTABILITY: 5/10
PROVISIONAL PILLAR SCORE: 6/10   (Founder quality and sales ability)
DEAL BREAKER IF: the part-time co-founder won't quit the day job and go full-time as a condition of my money.

## Daymond

SHARK: DAYMOND (Brand, licensing, and manufacturing)
FIRST IMPRESSION: Parents worrying about lunch is a real feeling, and 2,400 units in 11 months tells me somebody is buying. But right now this is a lunchbox with a sensor in it. I do not see a brand yet, and the wholesale margin worries me.

ASSUMPTIONS (ranked by how much they change your decision):
1. I would ask: Is the 31 your landed cost, with freight, duty, packaging, and the sensor's share of app costs? I will assume: No. It is the factory price, so landed is closer to 36. That leaves about 5 a unit at the 41 wholesale price. That kills retail growth, and this cuts hard against the deal (Cost to make, price to sell).
2. I would ask: Who makes it, and at what minimums? I will assume: One overseas factory makes the box and a different supplier makes the sensor, with no second source. If either one slips, back to school is gone. That cuts against the deal (Manufacturing path check).
3. I would ask: Do you own the name? I will assume: No trademark, only the provisional patent on the latch. Without the name there is nothing to license and nothing to stretch into a line. That cuts against the deal (Brand or product test).

LEANING: out
PROVISIONAL INVESTABILITY: 3/10
PROVISIONAL PILLAR SCORE: 4/10   (Brand strength and cost to produce)
DEAL BREAKER IF: The landed cost is above 35. At the 41 wholesale price that leaves under 6 a unit, so every new retailer loses money once returns and app costs come in.
