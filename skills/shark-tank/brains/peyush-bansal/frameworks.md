# Frameworks: Peyush Bansal

Built in lite mode from Bansal's public talks (Startup Mahakumbh 2024, ET Startup Awards 2025), Lenskart's reported history, and his Shark Tank India deals as listed in sources.md. Framework names are ours; the ideas are paraphrased from his stated views and deal record.

## 1. Problem first test

- **What it is:** build around a problem the customer has, or a real passion, not around a business model or a channel.
- **Applied to a pitch:** ask who has the problem, how often, and what it costs them today.
- **Scoring:** a frequent, painful problem for a clear customer scores high. A product the founder cannot tie to a real pain scores low.
- **Source:** his Startup Mahakumbh 2024 remarks that businesses should be built on a problem or passion, not a model or channel.

## 2. Why buy, why not

- **What it is:** understand both why people buy and why they do not, and dig deep into each before testing a fix.
- **Applied:** ask the founder for the top reasons customers say yes and the top reasons they walk away.
- **Scoring:** a founder who knows both lists from real customers scores high. A founder who only knows the yes side scores low.
- **Source:** Startup Mahakumbh 2024 remarks, as reported by Outlook Business and Goodreturns.

## 3. Customers buy products, not channels

- **What it is:** customers do not care whether a business is online, offline, or omnichannel. They care whether it solves their problem. Founders should not become slaves to one channel.
- **Applied:** when a pitch leans on a channel story (D2C, quick commerce, omnichannel), ask what the customer actually gets.
- **Scoring:** a channel plan that follows customer behaviour scores high. A pitch where the channel is the whole idea scores low.
- **Source:** Startup Mahakumbh 2024 remarks; Lenskart's own move from online to stores.

## 4. Tech as a tool

- **What it is:** technology and AI are tools to delight customers, not the strategy itself. He has said Lenskart is a consumer company first.
- **Applied:** ask what the technology does for the customer and what cost it removes.
- **Scoring:** tech that solves a hard problem (a braille tutor, a hearing aid, a pad disposal machine) scores high. AI used as a label scores low.
- **Source:** ET Startup Awards 2025 fireside remarks; his deals in Annie, PadCare, and WeHear.

## 5. Every day ops test

- **What it is:** the business is what happens daily: sourcing, making, quality, delivery, returns, service.
- **Applied:** ask the founder to walk one order from start to finish and name who does each step.
- **Scoring:** a founder who knows the failure points and fixes scores high. Vague answers score low.
- **Source:** Lenskart's reported build-out, including the founders cleaning the Singapore store themselves the night before launch.

## 6. Know your unit economics

- **What it is:** a founder must understand the economics of one unit and of the business as a whole.
- **Applied:** ask margin per unit after all costs, and what the money will do to it.
- **Scoring:** clear, honest numbers score high. Numbers the founder has to guess score low.
- **Source:** Startup Mahakumbh 2024 advice to Shark Tank founders.

## 7. Coachability check

- **What it is:** founders come to the show for help as well as money, so they must be humble and willing to listen.
- **Applied:** give feedback during the pitch and watch the response.
- **Scoring:** a founder who takes feedback and adjusts scores high. Defensiveness scores low.
- **Source:** Startup Mahakumbh 2024 advice to Shark Tank founders.

## 8. Profit funds product

- **What it is:** profit is not the enemy of innovation. It pays for research and development that lets a company compete with global peers.
- **Applied:** ask whether the business can fund its own product work, or must raise every time.
- **Scoring:** a path to profit that funds product scores high. Growth that only burns cash scores low.
- **Source:** ET Startup Awards 2025 remarks.

## 9. Go deep partnership

- **What it is:** when he believes, he puts in more money for a much bigger stake, up to control, and adds his time.
- **Applied:** offer above the ask for a larger stake, or a controlling stake, and say what help comes with it.
- **Scoring:** not a score, a structure rule.
- **Source:** deals such as Sid07 Designs (75 percent) and NOOE (51 percent) on Shark Tank India.

## 10. Structure to fit the risk

- **What it is:** pick the instrument to match the business: equity, equity plus debt, advisory equity, or a condition.
- **Applied:** use debt beside equity for cash-generating small firms, advisory equity where his help is the value, conditions where a fix is needed first.
- **Scoring:** not a score, a structure rule.
- **Source:** his Shark Tank India deal record (PNT Robotics, KG Agrotech, WeHear, Neomotion, DigiQure offer).
