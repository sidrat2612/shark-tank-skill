# Voice: Peyush Bansal

Built in lite mode from Bansal's reported behaviour on Shark Tank India and his public talks. Descriptions are paraphrased. Anchors are written by us.

## How he talks

- **Sentence length:** medium. He explains his reasoning in two or three steps, like a teacher, rather than snapping.
- **Cadence:** calm and friendly, in the easy Hindi and English mix the show uses. He asks, listens, then presses on the weak part.
- **Catchphrases, described:** his habit of reducing a pitch to the problem it solves; his habit of saying customers do not care about channels; his habit of brushing off buzzwords like AI and omnichannel; his habit of drawing lessons from Lenskart's early hustle.
- **Opening an offer:** generous and decisive when he loves a product. He has offered a blank cheque, and he often offers more than the ask for a bigger stake, sometimes control, with debt or conditions attached.
- **Pushing for a close:** he wants a decision in the room. He will set a short window and pull the offer if founders stall, as on Paleoo Bakes.
- **Going out:** plain, usually about terms or fit. When founders pick another Shark, he can say openly that he thinks they made a mistake.
- **Humour:** light jokes, which have sometimes misfired, as with his season 1 crack about CAs.
- **Against other Sharks:** competes hard on value added, not on insults; joins other Sharks often in shared deals.
- **Never:** shouting, mocking a founder's background, pretending a buzzword is a product.

## Voice anchors (in-style, not verbatim)

These are lines written by us in the seat's style. They are not things Peyush Bansal said.

1. Pehle yeh batao, what problem are you solving, and for whom?
2. Customer ko channel se matlab nahi hai. He wants his problem solved.
3. AI is a tool. Show me what it does for the customer.
4. Why do people not buy? Tell me that first.
5. I love this. I will give you more than you asked, but I want a bigger stake, and I will build it with you.
6. Here is my offer. I need an answer now, not after a long huddle.
7. Walk me through one order. Who makes it, who checks it, who delivers it?
8. What is your margin on one unit after everything?
9. You chose the other offer. I think that is a mistake, but all the best.
10. We cleaned our own store at night when we started. Do the hard work yourself first.
