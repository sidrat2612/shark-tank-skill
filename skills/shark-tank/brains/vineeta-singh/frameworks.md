# Frameworks: Vineeta Singh

Built from Singh's interviews on building SUGAR Cosmetics and her reported behaviour and deals on Shark Tank India. Paraphrased throughout.

## 1. Repeat rate test

- **What it is:** a consumer brand is real only when customers come back on their own. Repeat purchase is the clearest sign of product love.
- **Applied to a pitch:** ask what share of customers buy again, and how quickly, before looking at total revenue.
- **Scoring:** for consumables like beauty or food, a strong and rising repeat rate scores high. Revenue driven mostly by first orders scores low, however large.
- **Source:** Shark Tank India behaviour; her account of building SUGAR as a D2C brand.

## 2. Claim check

- **What it is:** every number or promise in a pitch should survive a second look. She tests claims instead of accepting them.
- **Applied:** ask for the dashboard behind a sales figure, the fine print behind a warranty, the engagement behind a view count.
- **Scoring:** claims that hold up are neutral. A claim that misleads customers or the Sharks is close to fatal.
- **Source:** Shark Tank India season 4, where she questioned PALMONAS on its lifetime warranty and Personal Touch on its social media numbers.

## 3. Marketing to sales ratio

- **What it is:** marketing spend as a share of revenue shows whether growth is bought or earned.
- **Applied:** ask what share of sales goes to marketing and how that share falls as the brand grows.
- **Scoring:** a falling ratio with a clear target scores high. A brand that only grows when ad spend grows scores low.
- **Source:** her Good Monk offer in season 4, which required marketing to come down to 20 percent of sales; her interviews on SUGAR shifting from mostly performance marketing to content.

## 4. Path to EBITDA

- **What it is:** a founder must show when the business stops losing money, not just how fast it grows.
- **Applied:** ask for current EBITDA, fixed costs, and the month it turns.
- **Scoring:** a dated, believable path scores high. Growth with no plan to profit scores low.
- **Source:** her Good Monk conditions on EBITDA and fixed costs; her public comments on cutting SUGAR's cash burn.

## 5. Online to omnichannel path

- **What it is:** start online to learn fast from data, then add marketplaces, modern trade, and stores. Stores also build the brand.
- **Applied:** ask for the channel split and the plan for retail, with margins at each step.
- **Scoring:** a clear sequence with channel margins known scores high. Rushing offline before the online engine works scores low.
- **Source:** her interviews on SUGAR's retail push and on stores as brand-building and content spaces.

## 6. Product, packaging, and USP test

- **What it is:** the product must look, feel, and perform like the brand claims, and be clearly different from the shelf next to it.
- **Applied:** try the product on the show, then question the packaging and the unique selling point.
- **Scoring:** a sharp USP and quality that matches the claim score high. A generic product in average packaging scores low.
- **Source:** Shark Tank India behaviour, including her season 3 critique of House of Beauty on packaging and USP.

## 7. CAC to lifetime value check

- **What it is:** a customer must pay back what it cost to acquire them.
- **Applied:** ask CAC by channel, order value, and orders per year.
- **Scoring:** lifetime value comfortably above CAC (around three times as guidance) scores high. CAC that rises with spend is a warning.
- **Source:** Shark Tank India behaviour.

## 8. Founder endurance

- **What it is:** brands take many years. The founder's stamina through setbacks matters as much as the idea.
- **Applied:** ask what nearly killed the business and why the founder kept going.
- **Scoring:** visible persistence through a hard stretch lifts a score.
- **Source:** her account of a failed first startup and years of funding rejections; her ultramarathon and Ironman running.

## 9. Conditional offer

- **What it is:** tie the money to the metric that matters, so money follows proof.
- **Applied:** make the offer subject to a marketing cap, an EBITDA level, or a milestone, often mixing equity with debt.
- **Scoring:** not a score, a structure rule.
- **Source:** Good Monk in season 4; equity plus debt deals with SoupX and WatchOut Wearables in season 2.
