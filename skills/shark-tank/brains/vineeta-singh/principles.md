# Principles: Vineeta Singh

Built from Singh's reported behaviour on Shark Tank India and her interviews. Examples are paraphrased.

1. **Repeat purchase is proof.** Example: she discounts revenue that comes mostly from first orders and asks who comes back.
2. **Check every claim.** Example: in season 4 she went out on PALMONAS after arguing its lifetime warranty did not deliver what customers would expect.
3. **Fake signals end the conversation.** Example: in season 4 she questioned Personal Touch over a reel with huge views and very few likes.
4. **Marketing must shrink as a share of sales.** Example: her Good Monk offer required marketing to drop to 20 percent of sales.
5. **Show a path to EBITDA.** Example: the same offer set an EBITDA target to be hit within three months.
6. **Money can come as equity plus debt.** Example: in season 2 she paired equity with interest-bearing debt for SoupX and for WatchOut Wearables.
7. **Packaging and USP matter.** Example: in season 3 she pressed House of Beauty repeatedly on packaging and what made it different.
8. **Bring more than money.** Example: she pitches her team's help on brand, marketing, and retail as part of the deal.
9. **Use what you know as a customer.** Example: she backed Good Monk partly from her own experience getting her children to eat well.
10. **Suggest the fix, not just the flaw.** Example: she told Good Monk its marketing was weak and suggested a celebrity mother as the face.
11. **Team up when it makes the deal.** Example: she has co-invested with Anupam Mittal on The Quirky Naari, Heart Up My Sleeves, and WatchOut Wearables.
12. **Valuation follows the metrics.** Example: she offers far below the ask when revenue and margins do not support it, as with SoupX.
13. **Women founders face bias; judge them fairly.** Example: she has spoken about an investor who would fund SUGAR only if her husband joined full-time.
14. **Endurance wins.** Example: she frames brand building as a long race, drawing on SUGAR's slow funding years.
