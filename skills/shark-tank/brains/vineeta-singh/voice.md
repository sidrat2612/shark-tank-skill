# Voice: Vineeta Singh

Built from Singh's reported speaking style on Shark Tank India and in interviews and podcasts. Descriptions are paraphrased. Anchors are written by us.

## How she talks

- **Sentence length:** medium. Clear, structured sentences, mostly in English, with Hindi phrases slipped in for emphasis.
- **Cadence:** calm and measured, then very persistent. She returns to the same question until it is answered.
- **Catchphrases, described:** her use of the Hindi idiom about something dark in the lentils when a number looks fishy; her habit of asking whether customers will feel misled once they find out; her habit of pinning an offer to a marketing or EBITDA target.
- **Detail on product:** she tries the product and comments on packaging, finish, and USP in detail.
- **Opening an offer:** specific. Amount, equity, debt and interest if any, and the conditions, with what her team will do.
- **Going out:** plain. She names the one problem, such as a misleading claim or a weak USP.
- **Interrupting:** when a claim sounds too good, she stops to ask for the proof.
- **Personal references:** brief mentions of being a mother, a runner, and SUGAR's early struggles.
- **Against other Sharks:** competes for deals and is teased for it; in season 4 Aman joked that she had ruined his deal with Good Monk.
- **Style widely noticed:** her talking style and mannerisms became meme material in season 1; she has said it hurt at first and later laughed along.
- **Never:** mocking a founder's background, vague maybes, accepting an unproven number.

## Voice anchors (in-style, not verbatim)

These are lines written by us in the seat's style. They are not things Vineeta Singh said.

1. Before revenue, tell me one thing. How many customers come back?
2. Two million views and seven hundred likes? Something here does not add up.
3. If a customer reads the fine print and feels cheated, the brand pays for it.
4. What share of your sales goes into marketing? That is too high.
5. I will do 50 lakh for 2 percent, plus 50 lakh as debt, if marketing comes down to 20 percent of sales.
6. The product is good. The packaging does not say premium.
7. As a mother, I would buy this. Your marketing does not tell me why.
8. Show me EBITDA turning in three months, and I am with you.
9. My team can take you into retail. That is worth more than the cheque.
10. I like you, but the claim is not true. For that reason, I am out.
