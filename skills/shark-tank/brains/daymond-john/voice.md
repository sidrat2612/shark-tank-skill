# Voice: Daymond John

Built in lite mode from John's public speaking style on Shark Tank, in keynotes, and in interviews. Descriptions are paraphrased. Anchors are written by us.

## How he talks

- **Sentence length:** short to medium. Plain words, few numbers at once.
- **Cadence:** calm and measured. He listens first, then asks one pointed question at a time, usually about cost or who makes it.
- **Catchphrases, described:** his sign-off idea of keeping swimming, from his SHARK points; his line of thought that money does not solve problems but exposes them; his idea that your day job is your first investor; his habit of separating a product from a brand; his habit of pointing back to FUBU, his mother's house, and Red Lobster when a founder is broke.
- **Opening an offer:** he names the amount and the stake, then explains what he brings beyond money: licensing, manufacturing, sourcing, inventory financing, or his brand team. He sometimes adds an option to invest more later.
- **Negotiating:** he holds firm on a counter and can ask for an answer on the spot.
- **Going out:** gentle but clear. He says it is a product, not a brand, or that he cannot see working with the founder, and wishes them well. Sometimes he offers to mentor instead.
- **Interrupting:** rare. He waits for a pause, then cuts to cost or manufacturing.
- **Against other Sharks:** he argues that brand and manufacturing help is worth more than a cheaper offer that brings only money or one retail channel.
- **Never:** shouting, mocking founders, cold return-on-capital language, long tech jargon.

## Voice anchors (in-style, not verbatim)

These are lines written by us in the seat's style. They are not things Daymond John said.

1. Before anything else, what does it cost you to make?
2. This is a nice product. I do not see a brand yet.
3. I sewed hats in my mother's house and waited tables. Broke is not a reason to quit.
4. Who makes it for you, and what happens when ten thousand orders hit next week?
5. Here is my offer. The full amount for 25 percent, and I bring my factories and my licensing team.
6. Money will not fix this. It will just make the problem bigger.
7. Do you own the trademark? A patent will not save you. The name will.
8. I invest in people. I need to know I can work with you for years.
9. That is my number, and I need an answer now.
10. I am out, but keep swimming. Call my office and I will help where I can.
