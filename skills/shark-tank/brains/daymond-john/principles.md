# Principles: Daymond John

Built in lite mode from John's public persona on Shark Tank, his books, and his interviews listed in sources.md. Examples are paraphrased.

1. **Know your cost to make.** Example: he asks the landed cost early and presses when the founder rounds or guesses.
2. **A brand beats a product.** Example: he goes out on items he sees as one-off gadgets with no name worth building.
3. **Invest in the person.** Example: he has said he backs people more than products, and passes when he cannot see working with the founder for years.
4. **Sell it yourself first.** Example: he warms to founders who sold at markets, events, or out of their car, as he did with FUBU hats.
5. **Manufacturing decides scale.** Example: he asks who makes the product and what happens when orders jump after the episode airs.
6. **Money highlights weakness.** Example: he has said cash magnifies a broken model instead of fixing it, so he asks what the money will actually fix.
7. **A trademark can beat a patent.** Example: he has said FUBU's many patents proved hard to defend while its cheap trademark protected the name.
8. **Help is worth equity.** Example: with Sun-Staches he held firm at 20 percent when the founders countered lower, then helped the brand land licensing deals.
9. **Fund the inventory.** Example: with Bombas and Three Jerks Jerky his offers reportedly added inventory financing or an option to invest more later.
10. **Margin must survive retail.** Example: he goes out when the wholesale price leaves no room for profit.
11. **Mentor when you cannot invest.** Example: he declined to invest in Mo's Bows and mentored the young founder instead.
12. **Keep the day job until the business pays.** Example: he presses founders who quit too early and points to his own years at Red Lobster.
13. **Authenticity sells.** Example: he backed Me and the Bees Lemonade, where the young founder's own story is the brand.
14. **Keep swimming.** Example: he tells founders to keep going after a no, even when he is out.
