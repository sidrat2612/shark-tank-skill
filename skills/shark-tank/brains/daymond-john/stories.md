# Stories: Daymond John

Built in lite mode from widely reported facts about John's companies and his best-known Shark Tank deals, checked against sources.md. Figures appear only where widely reported and consistent.

1. **Hats in Queens.** In 1992 he and friends started FUBU in Hollis, Queens, sewing tie-top hats in his mother's house and selling them on the street, while he waited tables at Red Lobster. Lesson: start small and sell it yourself.
2. **The second mortgage.** After dozens of banks refused a loan, his mother took a second mortgage on the house, and part of it became the factory. Lesson: being broke forces focus.
3. **The Samsung deal.** His mother placed a newspaper ad seeking investors, and it led to a deal with Samsung's textile division. Lesson: once demand arrives, you need a partner who can manufacture and finance it.
4. **The trade show orders.** FUBU took about 300,000 dollars in orders at the MAGIC apparel trade show in Las Vegas in the mid 1990s. Lesson: orders without production capacity are a crisis, not a win.
5. **LL Cool J and the Gap ad.** LL Cool J, from the same neighbourhood, wore a FUBU hat in a 1997 Gap commercial and slipped in the brand's slogan. Lesson: authentic ties to a community market a brand better than paid ads.
6. **FUBU at scale, then the stumble.** FUBU is reported to have reached billions in global sales, then overproduced, lost track of its numbers, and pulled back from the US market in the early 2000s. Lesson: brand heat does not replace inventory and cash discipline.
7. **The Shark Group.** He founded and runs The Shark Group, a brand consulting and marketing firm. Lesson: brand building is the help he sells, on and off the show.
8. **Bombas.** In season 6 he invested 200,000 dollars for 17.5 percent in the sock company with a donate-a-pair model, reportedly with inventory financing. It became one of the show's biggest successes. Lesson: a great product with a mission built into the brand can scale.
9. **Sun-Staches.** In season 6 he invested 300,000 dollars for 20 percent in the novelty sunglasses brand and helped it land a Marvel licensing deal. Lesson: licensing turns a novelty into a line.
10. **Bubba's-Q.** In season 5 he invested in former NFL player Al Bubba Baker's boneless rib company, and sales grew sharply after a restaurant chain deal. The relationship later broke down, and in 2023 a federal judge ruled the Bakers had breached a 2019 settlement by attacking him publicly. Lesson: hands-on help can turn into a fight over control.
11. **Me and the Bees Lemonade.** He invested 60,000 dollars for 25 percent in Mikaila Ulmer's lemonade brand, which later reached Whole Foods shelves. Lesson: an authentic founder story is a brand asset.
12. **Three Jerks Jerky.** In season 7 he invested 100,000 dollars for 15 percent, with an option to invest another 100,000 dollars for another 15 percent, beating offers from other Sharks. Lesson: he structures options when he expects to add more fuel.
13. **Mission Belt.** In season 4 he invested 50,000 dollars for 37.5 percent in the ratchet belt company. Lesson: a simple product with a clear brand and a cause can grow steadily.
14. **Mo's Bows.** He declined to invest in young Moziah Bridges' bow tie company and mentored him instead; the brand later reached Neiman Marcus and an NBA licensing deal. Lesson: sometimes the right offer is guidance, not money.
15. **Thyroid cancer.** In 2017 he was diagnosed with stage II thyroid cancer, had surgery, and urged people to get checked. Lesson: discipline about health is part of the grind.
