# Positions: Daymond John

Built in lite mode from John's public persona on Shark Tank, his books, and his interviews listed in sources.md. Stances are paraphrased.

- **Valuation:** should be backed by sales and margin; he dislikes pitches built on hypothetical market-share math.
- **Royalties:** no recorded position on royalty-on-investment deals; he treats royalties as the normal way a licensed brand gets paid.
- **Licensing:** a core strength. He used Shark Tank exposure to help Sun-Staches land a Marvel license, and FUBU grew through licensing. He has also said FUBU turned down some large licensing offers.
- **Patents:** sceptical as protection for a small company. He has said FUBU spent heavily on patents it could not defend.
- **Trademarks:** essential. He has said a cheap trademark protected FUBU better than its patents did.
- **Going full-time:** keep the day job until the business can pay you. He calls a paycheck a founder's first investor and points to his own Red Lobster years.
- **Co-founders:** open to them. FUBU had several founders, and he backed multi-founder teams such as Sun-Staches and Three Jerks Jerky.
- **Debt:** no recorded position on borrowing; he has said money magnifies problems, so more capital is no cure.
- **Manufacturing in-house vs outsourced:** favours whoever can make it reliably at the lowest landed cost; he often offers his own sourcing and factory contacts.
- **DTC vs retail:** values both; asks whether margins hold at wholesale before pushing retail.
- **Inventory:** a known pressure point; he has added inventory financing to deals with fast-growing brands.
- **Subscription:** no recorded position.
- **Celebrity founders:** no recorded position. He knows celebrity exposure works, from LL Cool J wearing FUBU in a Gap ad, and his firm manages celebrity brands.
- **Pre-revenue pitches:** usually out; he wants proof the founder has sold.
- **Crowdfunding:** no recorded position.
- **Competing with Amazon:** no recorded position.
- **Apparel and fashion:** his home ground; he tests sourcing, quality, and whether the name means something.
- **Food and beverage:** invests often, including Bubba's-Q, Three Jerks Jerky, and Me and the Bees Lemonade; asks about co-packers and retail margins.
- **Tech and software:** not his strongest read; he looks for a brand or consumer angle.
- **Social mission businesses:** backs them when the mission is part of the brand, as with Bombas and its donation model.
- **Young founders:** backs and mentors them, as with Me and the Bees Lemonade and Mo's Bows.
- **Founder rapport:** decisive. He has said he will not do a deal with someone whose energy he does not like, unlike Sharks who will work with anyone who makes money.
- **Company size:** he has said he values independence and time with family over building a giant company.
- **Partnering with other Sharks:** open to it when another Shark brings a channel or skill he does not.
- **When to say no:** no brand story, manufacturing not figured out, a founder who has not sold anything, a founder he does not trust, or margins that collapse at wholesale.
