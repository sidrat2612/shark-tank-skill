# Frameworks: Daymond John

Built in lite mode from John's books, his talks, and his public behaviour on Shark Tank. Paraphrased throughout.

## 1. Brand or product test

- **What it is:** a product is an item people buy once on price. A brand is an emotional promise people pay extra for, return to, and tell others about.
- **Applied to a pitch:** ask who the customer is, why they care about this name, and whether they would buy a second item under it.
- **Scoring:** a clear customer who identifies with the name scores high. An item that would sell just as well with no name on it scores low.
- **Source:** The Brand Within; repeated on Shark Tank across seasons.

## 2. Cost to make, price to sell

- **What it is:** the landed cost of one unit, the wholesale price, and the retail price, with the margin at each step.
- **Applied:** ask the cost to make, including freight and duty, then the wholesale and retail price.
- **Scoring:** as guidance, a physical product that retails at roughly four or five times landed cost leaves room for retailers and profit, and scores well. A product that cannot survive a wholesale discount scores low.
- **Source:** Shark Tank behaviour; his FUBU story in Display of Power.

## 3. Manufacturing path check

- **What it is:** a brand only scales if someone can make it reliably, at quality, at a cost that holds as volume grows.
- **Applied:** ask who makes it, where, the minimum order quantities, lead times, and what breaks at ten times the volume.
- **Scoring:** a tested factory relationship with known minimums scores high. Making everything by hand at home with no plan to move scores low, unless that is the brand.
- **Source:** Display of Power; Shark Tank behaviour.

## 4. Sold it yourself test

- **What it is:** the founder has sold the product hand to hand, at markets, events, or door to door, and knows what the customer says.
- **Applied:** ask where they have sold it in person and what objections they heard.
- **Scoring:** real units sold by the founder score high. A founder who has only made prototypes and slides scores low.
- **Source:** Display of Power; his account of selling FUBU hats on the street in Queens.

## 5. Licensing lens

- **What it is:** a strong brand name can be licensed to other manufacturers in new categories, earning royalties without owning the factory.
- **Applied:** ask whether the name could live on other products, and whether it is protected by a trademark.
- **Scoring:** a name with a loyal audience and room to stretch scores high. A name tied to one gadget scores low.
- **Source:** FUBU's growth through licensing; The Brand Within.

## 6. Power of broke

- **What it is:** limited money forces creativity, closeness to the customer, and discipline. Money can hide a broken model.
- **Applied:** ask what the founder built with little cash and how they would spend the investment.
- **Scoring:** scrappy proof on a small budget lifts the score. A plan that only works with large spending lowers it.
- **Source:** The Power of Broke.

## 7. SHARK points

- **What it is:** his five-part checklist from The Power of Broke and his keynotes: set a goal, do your homework, amor (love what you do), remember you are the brand, keep swimming.
- **Applied:** use it as a read on the founder: clear goals, knowledge of the market, passion, personal ownership of the brand, and persistence.
- **Scoring:** guidance, not a number. Weakness on homework or persistence matters most.
- **Source:** The Power of Broke.

## 8. People first

- **What it is:** he says he invests in people more than products, which is why he is called the People's Shark. The test is whether he would want to work with this founder for the next five or so years.
- **Applied:** read the founder's energy, honesty, and reasons for doing the business, alongside the numbers.
- **Scoring:** a founder he trusts and likes lifts a borderline deal. A founder he does not connect with can end a good one.
- **Source:** his interviews on why he is called the People's Shark; Shark Tank behaviour.

## 9. Goals on paper

- **What it is:** write specific goals down, with deadlines, and read them every day.
- **Applied:** ask the founder what the goals are for the next year and how they will be measured.
- **Scoring:** specific, dated goals score well. Vague ambition scores low.
- **Source:** the set a goal point of his SHARK points in The Power of Broke; Rise and Grind; his public talks.

## 10. Authentic community

- **What it is:** the strongest brands come from inside a community and speak to it, the way FUBU came from hip-hop culture.
- **Applied:** ask whether the founder is part of the community they sell to, and how that community found them.
- **Scoring:** a founder rooted in the customer base scores high. A brand aimed at a group the founder does not know scores low.
- **Source:** Display of Power; FUBU's history.

## 11. Influence, negotiation, relationships

- **What it is:** the three parts of Powershift: build a reputation that shows what you stand for, negotiate for a win on both sides, and nurture the connections you make.
- **Applied:** watch how the founder handles the negotiation in the tank and whether they leave room for a partner to win.
- **Scoring:** a founder who negotiates firmly but fairly scores well. One who fights every point or folds at once scores low.
- **Source:** Powershift.
