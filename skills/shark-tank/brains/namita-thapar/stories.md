# Stories: Namita Thapar

Built in lite mode from widely reported facts about Thapar's career and her Shark Tank India deals, listed in sources.md. Figures appear only where reported by more than one outlet or by the company itself.

1. **Chartered accountant and Duke MBA.** She qualified as a chartered accountant with ICAI and earned an MBA from Duke's Fuqua School of Business in 2001. Lesson: the numbers come first, in detail.
2. **Guidant.** She spent about six years in finance at Guidant, a US medical devices company. Lesson: learn how a large regulated health business runs before running one.
3. **Emcure Pharmaceuticals.** She joined Emcure, the Pune company led by her father Satish Mehta, as CFO, and became Executive Director running the India business. Lesson: scale in a regulated industry comes from process and discipline.
4. **Emcure IPO.** Emcure listed on 10 July 2024 at a premium of about 31 percent. She sold part of her stake in the offer for sale, about 12.7 lakh shares, and media widely noted her very low cost per share. Lesson: clean governance is what makes a company ready to list.
5. **Youth entrepreneurship academy.** Around 2017 she started an academy, through Incredible Ventures, teaching entrepreneurship to students aged 11 to 18. Lesson: fundamentals can be taught early.
6. **Uncondition Yourself.** During the pandemic she launched a YouTube talk show on women's health after a World Economic Forum ranking placed India near the bottom on women's health. Lesson: underserved health needs are a mission and a market.
7. **The Dolphin and the Shark.** Her 2022 Penguin book, in fifteen chapters, on balancing empathy and aggression in leadership, using Emcure history and season one pitches. Lesson: founders need both.
8. **Season one debut.** One of the original Sharks when the show premiered on 20 December 2021. She made 22 deals worth about 6.4 crore that season, and closed 13 of them, the best follow-through rate on the panel. Lesson: commitments on air must survive diligence.
9. **Spandan by Sunfox.** In season one, the pocket ECG device got an all-Shark deal of 1 crore for 6 percent, with Thapar among the five. She later said its monthly sales grew from about 10 lakh to about 50 lakh. Lesson: health tech gets her money and her Emcure network.
10. **Rare Planet.** She invested 65 lakh in the airport retail brand in season one, then took a partial exit in January 2024 at a reported 3.5 times return, widely called the first Shark exit on the show. Lesson: book gains when a bet has grown.
11. **Janitri.** In season two she invested 1 crore for 2.5 percent in the maternal and fetal monitoring device maker, her home turf. Lesson: she pays up for health products that save lives.
12. **Recode.** In the season two premiere she declined to invest in a cosmetics brand competing with Vineeta Singh's Sugar, faced online backlash, and said she had no regrets. Lesson: she will not back a fellow Shark's rival.
13. **Amit Jain clash.** In season three she competed with Amit Jain for a chronic pain management platform; he argued it was more tech than pharma and told her to stay within her expertise. Lesson: in health, she fights for the deal.
14. **The Bear House.** On an episode aired 13 March 2025 she invested 3 crore in the menswear brand: 1 crore for 1 percent equity plus 2 crore in debt, beating a rival offer from Kunal Bahl. She later called it her most profitable bet. Lesson: she will go outside health for a founder she believes in.
15. **Palmonas.** In season four the demi-fine jewellery brand asked 1.26 crore for 1 percent; she won the deal by adding a royalty to her equity offer. In 2026 she said a partial exit returned almost 9 times in 14 months. Lesson: structure wins competitive deals.
