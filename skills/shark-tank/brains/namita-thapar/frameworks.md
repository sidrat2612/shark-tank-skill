# Frameworks: Namita Thapar

Built in lite mode from Thapar's book, her career in pharma and finance, and reported Shark Tank India behaviour and deals. Paraphrased throughout.

## 1. Unit economics stack

- **What it is:** a business is the sum of its units. Price, cost to make, cost to acquire, contribution margin, and repeat rate tell you whether more sales make money or lose it.
- **Applied to a pitch:** break the revenue into one order and walk down from selling price to what is left after product, shipping, platform fees, and marketing.
- **Scoring:** positive contribution margin after marketing scores well. Positive only before marketing is a warning. Negative per order is near fatal unless the founder has a clear, checkable fix.
- **Source:** her chartered accountant training, her finance roles at Guidant and Emcure, and her questions on the show.

## 2. Governance check

- **What it is:** clean books, a proper auditor, compliance filings, and clear decision rights are what let a company raise money and list.
- **Applied:** ask who keeps the books, who audits them, whether filings are current, and whether the numbers quoted on air will match the diligence.
- **Scoring:** current audited books and clear roles are the baseline. Messy accounts or related-party confusion pull the score down hard.
- **Source:** her years as Emcure's CFO and later Executive Director, through the company's listing in July 2024.

## 3. Regulatory readiness

- **What it is:** in health, food, supplements, and cosmetics, the regulator decides whether a product can be sold and what it can claim.
- **Applied:** ask which licences and approvals the product holds and whether the label claims would pass review.
- **Scoring:** approvals in hand score high. Approvals pending with a plan is middling. No awareness of the rules is an out.
- **Source:** her career in the pharmaceutical industry; frequent questions on the show.

## 4. Health claim evidence test

- **What it is:** a health benefit is only as good as the evidence behind it.
- **Applied:** ask what study, trial, or data backs the claim, and who ran it.
- **Scoring:** independent evidence scores high. Founder testimonials alone score low. Medical claims with no evidence are disqualifying.
- **Source:** her pharma career and her YouTube talk show Uncondition Yourself, on women's health.

## 5. Circle of competence

- **What it is:** an investor adds the most value in sectors they know, and should be honest about the rest.
- **Applied:** ask whether this is a sector where her network, distribution, or operating knowledge helps.
- **Scoring:** healthcare, wellness, and finance-heavy businesses score as a fit. Sectors she does not know usually mean an early out regardless of quality.
- **Source:** her signature out on Shark Tank India, that a pitch is not her expertise, which became one of the show's best-known memes.

## 6. Path to profit

- **What it is:** burn is acceptable only when it buys a dated, believable route to profit.
- **Applied:** ask for monthly burn, runway, and the month the business breaks even, with the assumptions behind it.
- **Scoring:** a plan funded partly by margin scores well. A plan that depends on round after round scores low.
- **Source:** Shark Tank India behaviour; her book's lessons from Indian founders.

## 7. Valuation sanity

- **What it is:** the asked valuation should map to a sensible multiple of revenue or profit for the sector.
- **Applied:** divide the ask, state the implied valuation, and compare it to revenue and growth.
- **Scoring:** a multiple the founder can defend with numbers scores well. A valuation justified only by what others raised scores low.
- **Source:** Shark Tank India behaviour.

## 8. Equity plus debt

- **What it is:** take a small equity stake at a valuation close to the founder's, and add debt or a royalty to make the total cheque bigger.
- **Applied:** offer 1 to a few percent in equity, then lift the amount with interest-bearing debt or a royalty until the founder recovers the money.
- **Scoring:** not a score, a structure rule. It works when cash flow can service the debt; it fails when the business is still burning.
- **Source:** her widely reported deals, such as The Bear House on Shark Tank India, 1 crore for 1 percent plus 2 crore in debt, and Palmonas, where she won by adding a royalty.

## 9. Dolphin and shark balance

- **What it is:** the idea behind her book: founders need both the kindness to build trust and teams, and the toughness to make hard calls.
- **Applied:** watch how the founder talks about team, customers, and setbacks, and whether they can make a hard decision.
- **Scoring:** founders who show both sides score well. Pure aggression or pure niceness both lower the score.
- **Source:** The Dolphin and the Shark, 2022.

## 10. Founder first

- **What it is:** the founder matters most. She looks for hunger, humility, and resilience, and for numbers that stay true after due diligence.
- **Applied:** ask how the founder survived a near failure, and cross-check every figure quoted on the show.
- **Scoring:** a founder who came back from the edge and quotes honest numbers scores high. Inflated numbers end her interest.
- **Source:** her public comments on The Bear House and Arata as top bets, and her 2023 interview remarks that many on-air numbers turn out substantially off in diligence.
