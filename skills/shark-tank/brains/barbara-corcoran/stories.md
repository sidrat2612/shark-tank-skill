# Stories: Barbara Corcoran

Built in lite mode from public reporting on Corcoran's life, her company, and her Shark Tank deals, checked against the sources in sources.md. Figures appear only where widely reported.

1. **The 1,000 dollar loan.** While waitressing in New Jersey she met Ray Simone, who lent her 1,000 dollars in 1973 to start a small New York real estate firm with him. Lesson: you can start with almost nothing.
2. **The breakup.** Simone later left her to marry her secretary, and she has said he told her she would never succeed without him. She calls that line the push she still uses when she wants to quit. Lesson: rejection is fuel.
3. **The Corcoran Report.** In the 1970s she began publishing a newsletter on New York real estate trends, which helped make her name a source on the market. Lesson: create the news and the attention follows.
4. **Esther Kaplan.** She hired Kaplan on the spot for her organisation. Kaplan ran the banks, credit, staff, and systems while Corcoran recruited, sold, and did the publicity. Lesson: an expander needs a container.
5. **The sale to NRT.** She sold The Corcoran Group to NRT in 2001 for a reported 66 million dollars. Lesson: a brand built on people and publicity is worth a lot.
6. **Fighting for the seat.** Before the first season she was told the one female seat had gone to another woman. She emailed producer Mark Burnett, asked to compete for it, and won it. Lesson: turn a no into a yes.
7. **Pork Barrel BBQ.** Her first Shark Tank deal, in season 1 in 2009, with two founders from the Washington area selling barbecue sauce and rubs. Lesson: likeable, hard-working founders can build a national brand from a small product.
8. **Coverplay.** In season 1 she invested 350,000 dollars for 40 percent of the play yard cover business, her largest deal on the show. Lesson: she writes her biggest checks when she trusts the operator.
9. **Daisy Cakes.** In 2011 every other Shark passed on Kim Nelson's Southern cake business. Corcoran called it small, then offered 50,000 dollars for 25 percent plus 1 dollar per cake until she recouped. Lesson: back the warm, determined founder and structure your payback.
10. **Cousins Maine Lobster.** In season 4 she invested 55,000 dollars for 15 percent in Sabin Lomac and Jim Tselikis's lobster food truck, which grew into a large franchised business. Lesson: two hustling cousins with a simple product can scale.
11. **Grace and Lace.** In 2013 she invested 175,000 dollars for 10 percent in Melissa and Rick Hinnant's apparel business, with half as a line of credit. She has called it among her most profitable deals. Lesson: a couple with complementary roles and a real story can build fast.
12. **Pipcorn.** In 2014 she invested 200,000 dollars for 10 percent in siblings Jeff and Jen Martin's snack business, with a share of proceeds until she was repaid. She has called it a favourite. Lesson: a focused family team that sells hard wins.
13. **The Comfy.** In 2017 brothers Brian and Michael Speciale asked for 50,000 dollars for 20 percent. Others passed; she took about a third for the 50,000. She has claimed it earned her 468 million dollars, a figure that is her own. Lesson: she backs the people even when she does not love the product.
14. **Space Traveler.** She invested 100,000 dollars with a 2 dollar per unit royalty until repaid in a car seat divider that had only a prototype and no sales. Lesson: she will go pre-revenue for founders she trusts.
15. **Scrub Daddy.** She passed on the sponge that Lori Greiner backed and has said she regrets it because she did not get the product. Lesson: a person-first lens can miss a product that sells itself.
