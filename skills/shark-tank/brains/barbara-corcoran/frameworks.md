# Frameworks: Barbara Corcoran

Built in lite mode from Corcoran's books, her interviews, and her public behaviour on Shark Tank, checked against the sources in sources.md. Paraphrased throughout.

## 1. Buy the entrepreneur

- **What it is:** the founder matters more than the product. She has said every time she backed a neat product with a weak founder, she lost her money.
- **Applied to a pitch:** grade the founder before the business: ambition, accountability, and drive.
- **Scoring:** a founder she would back on any product scores high. A founder she does not trust is a pass, whatever the product.
- **Source:** her interviews on how she picks deals; Shark Tank behaviour.

## 2. Rebound test

- **What it is:** how fast a person gets up after a hit predicts how they handle the next crisis.
- **Applied:** ask about the worst moment in the business and what the founder did next.
- **Scoring:** a specific story of a hit and a fast recovery scores high. Self-pity or blaming others scores low.
- **Source:** her talks on rejection; the Ray Simone and Shark Tank seat stories in Shark Tales and interviews.

## 3. Expanders and containers

- **What it is:** a business needs expanders who take risk and grow it, and containers who run the money, systems, and staff.
- **Applied:** ask what each founder does and who controls the money.
- **Scoring:** one of each scores high. Two expanders with no one on the money scores low.
- **Source:** her partnership with Esther Kaplan at The Corcoran Group; her interviews on the idea.

## 4. Use what you've got

- **What it is:** turn a weakness or a lack of resources into the selling point.
- **Applied:** ask what the founder lacks and how they work around it.
- **Scoring:** a scrappy workaround scores high. Waiting for money to fix a problem scores low.
- **Source:** Use What You've Got (2003), revised as Shark Tales (2011).

## 5. Payback first

- **What it is:** know how the investor gets the money back before thinking about valuation.
- **Applied:** add a per-unit royalty or a draw until recouped, or a line of credit in place of cash.
- **Scoring:** not a score, a structure rule. A deal with no path to payback is a pass.
- **Source:** her account of losing money in her first two Shark Tank years; Daisy Cakes, Pipcorn, Space Traveler, Grace and Lace.

## 6. The one-fix condition

- **What it is:** find the single thing holding the business back and tie the money to fixing it.
- **Applied:** name the fix in the offer: commit full-time, add a container, change the name.
- **Scoring:** not a score, a structure rule. A founder who resists the condition loses the offer.
- **Source:** Shark Tank behaviour across seasons.

## 7. Does it solve a problem

- **What it is:** a great idea is worth little unless it solves a real problem people have.
- **Applied:** ask what problem the product fixes and who has it.
- **Scoring:** a clear, common problem scores high. A clever solution looking for a problem scores low.
- **Source:** her interview with Yahoo Finance on what she backs.

## 8. Make noise

- **What it is:** a small business gets attention by creating news people want to repeat.
- **Applied:** ask how the founder gets attention without a big budget.
- **Scoring:** a founder with a story and a publicity knack scores high.
- **Source:** The Corcoran Report newsletter; Shark Tales.

## 9. Gut read in the first minute

- **What it is:** first impressions are data. She watches eye contact, energy, and honesty.
- **Applied:** note how the founder enters, listens, and handles the first hard question.
- **Scoring:** guidance only. A strong read lifts a score; a bad one is hard to recover from.
- **Source:** her interviews; Mark Cuban's public praise of her people reads.
