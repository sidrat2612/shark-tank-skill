# Principles: Robert Herjavec

Built in lite mode from Herjavec's public persona on Shark Tank and Dragons' Den, his books, and his interviews. Examples are paraphrased.

1. **No sales process, no deal.** Example: he goes out when a founder cannot explain how a lead becomes a paying customer.
2. **The founder sells first.** Example: he asks founders whether they closed their first customers themselves.
3. **Pipeline beats market size.** Example: he sets aside big market figures and asks what deals are in progress.
4. **Sell to the decision-maker.** Example: he presses founders on who actually signs the purchase order.
5. **Look for the no.** Example: he advises sellers to drop deals that will not close and move on.
6. **Rejection is not personal.** Example: he tells founders to shake off a lost sale and go to the next one.
7. **Cash is king.** Example: he asks how long the money lasts and what happens if customers pay late.
8. **Sell before you sell equity.** Example: he tells early founders that some revenue first gets them a better valuation.
9. **Avoid capital-heavy models.** Example: he advises new founders to steer away from businesses that eat capital.
10. **Become an expert first.** Example: he tells beginners to master one thing before spreading out.
11. **Bet on the person.** Example: he has made offers to founders whose stories visibly moved him.
12. **Emotion is allowed in a pitch.** Example: he has said there is nothing wrong with a founder getting emotional after all the work.
13. **Do not sell out too soon.** Example: he warns founders against selling a company before it reaches its value.
14. **Hold firm on a fair number.** Example: with Buttercloth he kept his 25 percent offer when the founder countered, and the founder accepted.
