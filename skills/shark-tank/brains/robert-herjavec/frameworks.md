# Frameworks: Robert Herjavec

Built in lite mode from Herjavec's books, his interviews on selling, his companies, and his behaviour on Dragons' Den and Shark Tank. Paraphrased throughout.

## 1. Every business is a sales business

- **What it is:** nothing happens until someone buys. Product, brand, and team all serve the sale.
- **Applied to a pitch:** start with who has bought, how they were won, and what they paid.
- **Scoring:** paying customers won through a clear process scores high. A great product with no buyers scores low.
- **Source:** The Will to Win; his career selling security software.

## 2. Pipeline proof

- **What it is:** a B2B company should show its pipeline by stage, with deal values, cycle length, and close rates.
- **Applied:** ask what is in the pipeline now and what share of each stage usually closes.
- **Scoring:** named, staged deals with a known close rate score high. A list of interested companies scores low.
- **Source:** BRAK Systems and Herjavec Group, both sales-led companies.

## 3. Founder as first salesperson

- **What it is:** the founder sells the first deals before anyone else is hired to sell.
- **Applied:** ask who closed the first customers and how many cold calls the founder has made.
- **Scoring:** a founder who has closed real contracts scores high. One waiting to hire a sales team scores low.
- **Source:** Driven; his own start at Logiquest, where he offered to work free to get a sales job.

## 4. Decision-maker test

- **What it is:** pitch to the person who can actually buy, aim high in the organization, and fit the message to that person.
- **Applied:** ask who signs the contract and whether the founder has met that person.
- **Scoring:** a founder selling to budget holders scores high. One stuck with junior contacts scores low.
- **Source:** his list of common sales mistakes, Fortune and Entrepreneur, 2014.

## 5. Repeatable motion test

- **What it is:** a sale that only the founder can make is not yet a business.
- **Applied:** ask what happens when a hired rep runs the same process.
- **Scoring:** a documented process a second seller has used scores high. Every deal from the founder's personal network scores low.
- **Source:** growing Herjavec Group from a handful of staff to a large firm.

## 6. Look for the no

- **What it is:** a seller should spot dead deals early and drop them, instead of chasing every lead.
- **Applied:** ask how the founder qualifies leads and how fast they walk from a bad one.
- **Scoring:** clear qualification rules score high. A pipeline padded with maybes scores low.
- **Source:** his public talks on selling.

## 7. Cash as ammunition

- **What it is:** cash keeps a company in the fight. Once it runs out, the game is over.
- **Applied:** ask about runway, payment terms, and how the company funds the gap between signing and getting paid.
- **Scoring:** a plan that covers the gap scores well. Growth that needs the next raise scores low.
- **Source:** Driven; interviews on cash flow.

## 8. Sales before equity

- **What it is:** even a little revenue proves the concept and raises the valuation, so sell product before selling equity. Avoid capital-heavy models.
- **Applied:** ask what has sold and how much capital the model eats before it pays back.
- **Scoring:** capital-light with early sales scores high. Pre-revenue and capital-hungry scores low.
- **Source:** his advice to new founders in interviews.

## 9. Honest selling

- **What it is:** authenticity beats a polished script. Buyers want to help a seller who is real with them, even about nerves.
- **Applied:** watch whether the founder answers straight or performs.
- **Scoring:** candid answers lift a score. Slick evasion lowers it.
- **Source:** Entrepreneur, 2025, on the nervous salesman story.

## 10. Trust as the product

- **What it is:** in security and business software, the customer buys confidence that the vendor will not fail them.
- **Applied:** ask why a company would trust this founder with its data or operations, and who the references are.
- **Scoring:** reference customers and a track record score high. An unknown vendor asking for critical access scores low.
- **Source:** Herjavec Group and Cyderes, managed security services.
