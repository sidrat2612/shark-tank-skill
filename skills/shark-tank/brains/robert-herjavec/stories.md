# Stories: Robert Herjavec

Built in lite mode from widely reported facts about Herjavec's life, his companies, and his Shark Tank deals. Figures appear only where widely reported.

1. **Arriving from Yugoslavia.** Born in Varaždin in 1962, he came to Canada in 1970 at age eight after his father was jailed for criticizing the government. The family landed in Halifax with very little and lived in a friend's basement in Toronto. Lesson: hunger and effort can outrun a lack of money.
2. **Odd jobs.** After graduating from the University of Toronto in 1984 with degrees in English and political science, he waited tables, delivered newspapers, and worked on film sets. Lesson: a non-technical start does not block a tech career.
3. **Working for free at Logiquest.** With no sales or computer experience, he offered to work free for six months at Logiquest, which sold IBM mainframe emulation boards. He rose to general manager, then was fired in 1990. Lesson: earn the chance to sell, then prove it.
4. **BRAK Systems.** Founded in 1990 from his basement, an integrator of internet security software, sold to AT&T Canada in March 2000 for a reported 30.2 million dollars. Lesson: security buyers pay for trust.
5. **Ramp Network.** After BRAK he was VP of sales at Ramp Network, later sold to Nokia. Lesson: a top seller adds value wherever he lands.
6. **Herjavec Group.** Founded in 2003 with three employees and about 400,000 in sales, it grew to roughly 200 million in annual revenue. Apax Partners bought a majority stake in 2021. Lesson: recurring security contracts compound.
7. **Cyderes.** Herjavec Group merged with Fishtech Group and was rebranded Cyderes in 2022, with him as CEO. He stepped down as CEO in 2024. Lesson: scale wins in a consolidating market.
8. **Dragons' Den and Shark Tank.** An original Dragon on CBC's Dragons' Den, then one of the original Sharks on ABC, and from 2023 a Shark on Shark Tank Australia. Lesson: he judges pitches with an operator's eye across three countries.
9. **Tipsy Elves.** 100,000 for 10 percent in season 5. The ugly holiday sweater brand is widely cited as his most successful Shark Tank deal. Lesson: founders who sell hard online into a clear season can scale.
10. **ChordBuddy.** 175,000 for 20 percent in season 3 in Travis Perry's guitar learning device, which grew strongly after the show. Lesson: a simple product with a clear buyer and a founder who sells it.
11. **Sand Cloud.** The founders asked 200,000 for 8 percent; he gave 200,000 for 15 percent in season 8, and the beach towel brand grew fast. Lesson: he pays for founders who can sell, but on his terms.
12. **Buttercloth.** The founder asked 250,000 for 10 percent; he held at 250,000 for 25 percent in season 10, and the founder accepted. Lesson: hold a fair number.
13. **Breathometer.** All five Sharks put in 1 million for 30 percent in season 5. The FTC later found the company knowingly made false accuracy claims, and it shut down. Lesson: a full panel can be wrong when nobody checks the product.
14. **Zero Pollution Motors.** In 2015 he shook hands on a 5 million investment in a compressed-air car company, which would have been a record. The deal fell apart in due diligence. Lesson: a handshake on TV is not a closed deal.
15. **Red Dress Boutique.** He and Mark Cuban made a joint 1.2 million offer for the online boutique in season 6; reports differ on the final equity. Herjavec later backed out and Cuban stayed in. Lesson: on-air deals change after the cameras stop.
