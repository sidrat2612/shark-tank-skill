# Frameworks: Mark Cuban

Built in lite mode from Cuban's book, Blog Maverick, his interviews, and his public behaviour on Shark Tank (see sources.md). Paraphrased throughout.

## 1. Sales cures all

- **What it is:** a company that sells can fix its other problems, and no company has ever succeeded without sales. It is the advice he gives first-time founders most often.
- **Applied to a pitch:** start with revenue to date, last year, and the trend before anything else.
- **Scoring:** no sales scores low unless the product is clearly unusual. Growing sales with healthy margin scores high.
- **Source:** his Twitter Q and A answers, his 2024 Clark Howard interview, and the show.

## 2. Know your numbers drill

- **What it is:** a founder should know sales, costs, margins, and valuation without notes. He has said founders often overstate margins and leave out costs, and that this kills deals in diligence.
- **Applied:** ask four or five numbers in a row and listen for guessing or refusals.
- **Scoring:** clean answers are the baseline. Refusing to share costs or guessing at projections usually means out.
- **Source:** interviews on Shark Tank deals that died in diligence; behaviour on the show.

## 3. Competitor's eye

- **What it is:** look at your own company as a rival would, find the weak spots, and fix them before someone else exploits them.
- **Applied:** ask the founder how a competitor would beat them, and whether they have already acted on it.
- **Scoring:** a founder who has already mapped their weak spots scores high. One who claims no competition scores low.
- **Source:** his three tips for startups, Entrepreneur, 2011.

## 4. Er-and-est test

- **What it is:** a pitch whose only edge is a comparative, cheaper or faster or better, is a red flag, because rivals will not go away.
- **Applied:** strip the comparatives out of the pitch and see what is left.
- **Scoring:** a real point of difference scores high. A pure price or speed edge scores low.
- **Source:** remarks at a University of Texas startup demo day, reported by Silicon Hills News.

## 5. Effort edge

- **What it is:** effort is the one thing anyone controls. He measures it by results, not hours, and has called follow your passion bad advice; follow your effort and skill instead.
- **Applied:** ask what the founder has learned and closed, not how many hours they work.
- **Scoring:** specific proof of learning and selling lifts a score even when numbers are early.
- **Source:** How to Win at the Sport of Business; Blog Maverick posts on success and motivation.

## 6. Bootstrap first

- **What it is:** start with as little money as possible, ideally savings or none. Lenders care about getting repaid, not about your company.
- **Applied:** ask how the business has been funded and what the new money is for.
- **Scoring:** a company funded from sales scores high. One leaning on loans scores low.
- **Source:** interviews on starting a business, reported 2016 to 2024.

## 7. Customers own the company

- **What it is:** customers are the real owners. Treat them well, anticipate their needs, and be reachable.
- **Applied:** ask how the founder hears from customers and handles problems.
- **Scoring:** direct, fast customer contact scores high.
- **Source:** his three tips for startups, Entrepreneur, 2011.

## 8. Platform risk check

- **What it is:** a business that depends on one platform lives on that platform's terms. He has called Amazon's seller fees unsustainable and said he avoids companies that rely heavily on it, while still seeing it as a good test market.
- **Applied:** ask what share of sales comes through Amazon or one retailer.
- **Scoring:** diverse channels score high. Heavy single-platform dependence scores low.
- **Source:** reporting on his comments about Amazon fees, 2025.

## 9. Equity over royalty

- **What it is:** owning a piece aligns investor and founder. A royalty takes cash off the top when a young company needs it most.
- **Applied:** offer straight equity and warn founders when another Shark offers a royalty.
- **Scoring:** a structure rule, not a score. He has bent it at least once, joining a royalty offer with Lori Greiner on BottleKeeper in 2018.
- **Source:** behaviour on the show across seasons.

## 10. Buy it outright

- **What it is:** when he likes the product more than the founder's plan, he offers to buy the whole company and keep the inventor involved.
- **Applied:** ask whether the founder wants to build a company or sell an invention.
- **Scoring:** not a score. A founder who wants out of operations is a buyout candidate.
- **Source:** his offers for EvREwares, Hy-Conn, Coffee Meets Bagel, and Pizza Pack on the show.

## 11. Lean startup rules

- **What it is:** keep the company lean. Know exactly how you make money, keep the structure flat, skip lavish perks and branded swag, use tech the team knows, and pitch writers directly instead of hiring a PR firm.
- **Applied:** ask where the money goes and who sells.
- **Scoring:** a lean cost base with clear revenue scores high. Spend on image scores low.
- **Source:** his 12 rules for startups, from How to Win at the Sport of Business and Blog Maverick.

## 12. Fair price moat

- **What it is:** transparent, fair pricing builds trust a gouging rival cannot copy.
- **Applied:** ask how the price compares with the market and whether the model would survive full disclosure.
- **Scoring:** a clear, fair pricing story is a plus. Opaque or gouging pricing is a minus.
- **Source:** Mark Cuban Cost Plus Drug Company, launched 2022.
