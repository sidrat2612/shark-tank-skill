# Stories: Mark Cuban

Built in lite mode from verified public reporting on Cuban's companies and his best-known Shark Tank deals and misses (see sources.md). Figures appear only where several outlets agree.

1. **Early jobs and MicroSolutions.** After Indiana University (1981) he worked at Mellon Bank, tended bar in Dallas, and was fired from a software sales job within a year. He then co-founded MicroSolutions, a systems integrator, and sold it to CompuServe in 1990 for about 6 million. Lesson: learn the product better than the customer and go sell it yourself.
2. **Broadcast.com.** He and Todd Wagner joined Audionet in 1995 and renamed it Broadcast.com in 1998. He has said they expected an offer of about 75 million from Microsoft that never came. Yahoo bought the company in 1999 for about 5.7 billion in stock. Lesson: an early no can lead to a far bigger yes.
3. **Dallas Mavericks.** He bought a majority stake from Ross Perot Jr. in January 2000 for 285 million and won the 2011 NBA title. The NBA has fined him well over a million dollars across many incidents, mostly for criticizing referees and the league. In December 2023 the league approved the sale of control to the Adelson and Dumont families at a reported valuation of about 3.5 billion or more; he kept a minority stake. Lesson: he runs the fan experience like a business, and his mouth has a price.
4. **The 2018 workplace investigation.** An outside probe found years of misconduct by a former Mavericks CEO and weak management controls. Cuban was not cited for wrongdoing but agreed to give 10 million to groups promoting women in leadership and fighting domestic violence. Lesson: the owner answers for the culture even when he did not cause the harm.
5. **Media bets.** He co-founded HDNet in 2001, which became AXS TV; Anthem Sports took a majority stake in 2019. With Wagner he built 2929 Entertainment, which owns Magnolia Pictures and bought Landmark Theatres in 2003. Lesson: he likes owning the pipe and the content together.
6. **Cost Plus Drugs.** Radiologist Alex Oshmyansky sent him a cold email, and Cuban funded the company. It launched publicly in January 2022 selling generics at cost plus a 15 percent markup and a flat pharmacy fee. Lesson: published, fair pricing can be the whole moat.
7. **Red Swoosh and the Uber miss.** He backed Travis Kalanick's Red Swoosh, which Akamai bought in 2007. Around 2009 Kalanick offered him a 250,000 stake in Uber at a 10 million valuation. Cuban countered at 5 million over regulatory worries and never heard back. He calls it his biggest miss. Lesson: his risk read can be right about the fight and wrong about the prize.
8. **Tower Paddle Boards.** Season 3, 2012. Stephan Aarstol asked 150,000 for 10 percent; Cuban took 30 percent for the 150,000. The company says it has paid him over a million in dividends. Lesson: a lean online seller can return real cash, not just paper value.
9. **Simple Sugars.** 2013. He put 100,000 into teenage founder Lani Lazzari's scrub company for 33 percent and later named her his favorite entrepreneur for her drive. Lesson: he bets on relentless founders, even very young ones.
10. **Ten Thirty One Productions.** Season 5, 2013. Melissa Carbone asked 2 million for 10 percent; Daymond John offered 2 million for 40 percent; Cuban took her counter of 2 million for 20 percent. It was the largest deal on the show at the time. Lesson: he pays up for an operator he trusts.
11. **Breathometer.** Season 5, 2013. All five Sharks backed the smartphone breathalyzer. In 2017 the FTC said its accuracy claims lacked proof, and the company agreed to refund buyers. Cuban says he lost about 500,000 and blames the founder, who disputes his account. Lesson: test the tech claim, not just the pitch.
12. **Rugged Maniac.** Season 5, 2014. He offered 1.5 million for 25 percent; the founders pushed and he paid 1.75 million. In 2018 New Media Investment Group bought 80 percent of the obstacle race for 10.4 million, and Cuban sold his whole stake, roughly doubling his money. Lesson: an event business with repeat customers can exit cleanly.
13. **BeatBox Beverages.** Season 6. He put in 1 million for a party wine drink, most outlets say for a third of the company. The brand later grew into a very large seller. Lesson: a loyal community plus relentless selling gets his biggest checks.
14. **Dude Wipes.** Season 7, 2015. He went out, then jumped back in when Robert and Kevin bid, and took 25 percent for 300,000. He calls it his best Shark Tank investment. Lesson: a clear brand in a big, ignored category can beat the tech deals.
15. **Deals that never closed, and ones that got away.** He offered to buy Hy-Conn outright for 1.25 million, then the deal died and the founder publicly blamed his ego. Plated's founders tried to renegotiate after the show and he walked. A 2018 BottleKeeper deal with Lori, 1 million for 5 percent plus a royalty, fell through in diligence. Coffee Meets Bagel turned down his 30 million offer for the whole company, and Doorbot, which he passed on, became Ring and sold to Amazon in 2018. Lesson: the handshake on TV is the start of the deal, not the end.
