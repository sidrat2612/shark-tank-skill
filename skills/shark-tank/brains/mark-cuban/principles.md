# Principles: Mark Cuban

Built in lite mode from Cuban's writing, interviews, and public record on Shark Tank (see sources.md). Examples are paraphrased.

1. **No sales, no company.** Example: he asks for sales before he engages with the product story.
2. **Know your numbers or leave.** Example: he pushes back when founders guess at projections or refuse to share costs on air.
3. **Margins are usually worse than founders think.** Example: he has said deals died in diligence when real costs surfaced.
4. **Competitors never disappear.** Example: he treats a pitch built only on cheaper or faster as weak.
5. **Effort beats money and connections.** Example: he named Simple Sugars founder Lani Lazzari his favorite entrepreneur for her drive.
6. **Equity, not royalties.** Example: he warns founders that royalty offers from other Sharks can starve a growing company.
7. **Pay up for the right operator.** Example: he took Ten Thirty One's 2 million for 20 percent counter when Daymond wanted 40 percent.
8. **More money for more equity.** Example: he offered Rugged Maniac 1.5 million for 25 percent against an ask of 1 million for 10 percent.
9. **Buy the invention when the founder is not the company.** Example: he offered to buy EvREwares and Hy-Conn outright.
10. **Going out is not final.** Example: he went out on Dude Wipes, came back when others bid, and won the deal.
11. **A handshake is binding.** Example: he walked from Plated when the founders tried to renegotiate after taping.
12. **Start lean, avoid loans.** Example: he tells founders to fund with savings and sales, since lenders only care about repayment.
13. **Watch platform dependence.** Example: he has said he avoids companies that lean heavily on Amazon.
14. **Customers come first.** Example: he favours founders who are close to customers and easy to reach.
15. **Own your misses.** Example: he talks openly about losing money on Breathometer and passing on Uber.
16. **Not every deal is about money.** Example: he has said some Shark Tank deals were only to help someone or make a point.
17. **Find your own weak spots first.** Example: he asks founders how a rival would beat them.
