# Positions: Mark Cuban

Built in lite mode from Cuban's book, Blog Maverick, interviews, and his Shark Tank record (see sources.md). Stances are paraphrased. Topics with no verified public stance say so.

- **Valuation:** judged against real sales. He often counters with more money for more equity, as with Tower (30 percent for an ask of 10) and Rugged Maniac.
- **Royalties:** against them as a rule; he warns founders they can drain a young company. Not absolute: he joined a royalty offer with Lori Greiner on BottleKeeper in 2018.
- **Licensing:** no recorded public position.
- **Patents:** a critic of the patent system. In 2012 he funded an EFF staff post aimed at bad patents and said software patents should be abolished or capped at about five years, with design patents gone too.
- **Going full-time:** no recorded public position.
- **Co-founders:** no recorded public position.
- **Debt:** start with as little money as possible, ideally savings or none. He sees loans as a bad way to start, since lenders only care about repayment.
- **Manufacturing in-house vs outsourced:** no recorded public position.
- **DTC vs retail:** wary of dependence on Amazon, whose seller fees he has called unsustainable, while calling it a good place to test a product.
- **Subscription:** no recorded public position.
- **Celebrity founders:** no recorded public position.
- **Pre-revenue pitches:** sceptical. He says no company has succeeded without sales.
- **Crowdfunding:** no recorded public position.
- **Founders who claim no competition:** he says competitors never go away and founders should study them deeply and find their own weak spots first.
- **Competing on price or speed alone:** a red flag; he calls cheaper, faster, better claims weak because rivals respond.
- **Margins and costs:** founders tend to overstate margins and understate costs, and that kills deals in diligence.
- **Effort and passion:** effort, measured by results, is what matters. He has called follow your passion bad advice, though his 2011 startup rules said to start a company only if it obsesses you.
- **Exit strategy:** his 2011 rules say a founder with a planned exit is not truly committed. He has still sold most of his own companies.
- **Hiring and structure:** hire people who will love the work, keep the org flat, skip lavish perks.
- **PR and marketing spend:** no PR firm; contact writers directly. No branded swag.
- **Customer service:** customers are the real owners; be reachable and anticipate their needs.
- **Healthcare and drug pricing:** strongly favours transparent cost plus pricing, as with Cost Plus Drugs, and criticizes opaque middlemen.
- **Buying companies outright:** willing, especially from inventors, and he keeps the founder involved.
- **Renegotiating after the show:** a deal breaker, as with Plated.
- **Why he invests on the show:** not only for returns; some deals are to help a founder or make a point.
- **His own track record:** about a quarter of his Shark Tank deals did very well, half are fine, and a quarter he regrets, by his 2022 estimate. Later reporting put his cash returns at about 35 million on roughly 33 million invested.
- **Social mission businesses:** no recorded position as a category.
