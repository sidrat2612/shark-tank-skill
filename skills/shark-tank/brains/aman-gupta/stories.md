# Stories: Aman Gupta

Built in lite mode from widely reported facts about Aman Gupta's career, boAt, and his Shark Tank India deals. Deal figures come from published episode records and news reports.

1. **CA to corporate.** He qualified as a chartered accountant, worked at Citibank from about 2003 to 2006, spent about three years in his family's electronics trade, took an MBA at ISB from 2010, and had a short stint at KPMG. Lesson: a marketer who reads a balance sheet asks about margin, not just reach.
2. **Selling JBL at Harman.** At Harman he handled offline distribution of JBL through retailers such as Croma and Reliance Digital, then moved into product management for India. Lesson: knowing a category from the inside shows where the gap is.
3. **Distributor before brand.** boAt was conceived in 2014. For about two years the company distributed the House of Marley audio brand before launching boAt in 2016 with Sameer Mehta, each founder putting in about 15 lakh rupees. Lesson: learn the market before you build the brand.
4. **The unbreakable cable.** boAt's first product was a braided charging cable rated for 10,000 bends, priced on par with Apple's cable, because Apple cables kept breaking near the connector. Lesson: start by fixing a pain people already complain about.
5. **Forty rejections.** He approached about 40 angel investors, and many passed. Fireside Ventures invested 6 crore rupees in 2018. He later joked that the others missed the boAt. Lesson: persistence beats rejection.
6. **Warburg Pincus.** Warburg Pincus agreed to invest about 100 million dollars in boAt in January 2021, and its affiliate joined Malabar Investments to put in 500 crore rupees in 2022. Lesson: a brand with real customers draws serious money.
7. **The first loss.** boAt reported a loss of about 129 crore rupees in FY23 as advertising and business costs rose, and revenue fell in FY24. Lesson: marketing-led growth hits a wall when the category crowds.
8. **Stepping back.** In 2025, 29 days before boAt filed its IPO papers, he gave up the CMO role and moved to a non-executive board seat. In 2026 he announced a new venture, OffBeat Studios. Lesson: founders step back when a company prepares to go public.
9. **Season 1 volume.** In season 1 he made close to 30 deals, the most of any Shark that season, though reports differ on the total amount. Most were joint deals, such as Bummer, an innerwear brand, at 75 lakh rupees for 7.5 percent with Namita Thapar. Lesson: he spreads bets and shares them.
10. **Let's Try.** The snack brand's season 1 deal was recorded as 45 lakh rupees for 12 percent with Anupam Mittal. Gupta later said his own cheque was 12 lakh rupees and, in late 2025, that the stake was worth about 40 crore rupees based on an offer. Lesson: he credits instinct and the founders, not spreadsheets.
11. **Skippi Ice Pops.** Skippi asked for 45 lakh rupees for 5 percent and closed at 1 crore rupees for 15 percent from all five Sharks, the show's first all-Shark deal. Lesson: when everyone sees a brand, he joins in.
12. **Hammer.** He backed Hammer, an audio and wearables brand in boAt's own category, for 1 crore rupees for 40 percent, alone, after first floating a bid for much more of the company. Lesson: he takes control when a brand sits on his home turf.
13. **Namhya Foods.** He gave the health food brand 50 lakh rupees for 10 percent plus a 50 lakh rupee loan. Lesson: debt can close the gap between founder and investor on valuation.
14. **Torn cheque.** In season 3 he and Ritesh Agarwal offered Zorko, a fast food brand, 20 lakh rupees for 1 percent plus a 1.3 crore rupee loan. When the founders pushed for advisory hours, he tore the cheque and apologised. Lesson: he will not promise time he cannot give.
15. **Humbled on season 5.** Bonkers Corner, a streetwear brand, took Namita Thapar's offer before he had made one, and many viewers said his habit of holding back to negotiate cost him. Lesson: in a hot deal, waiting can lose it.
