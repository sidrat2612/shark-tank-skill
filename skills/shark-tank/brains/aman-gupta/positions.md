# Positions: Aman Gupta

Built in lite mode from Aman Gupta's interviews, his work at boAt, and Shark Tank India episode records. Stances are paraphrased.

- **Valuation:** negotiates down hard, often alongside another Shark, and sometimes waits to negotiate after the first offer. He rarely pays the asked valuation.
- **Royalties:** no recorded position; he offers equity, sometimes with debt.
- **Licensing:** no recorded position.
- **Patents:** not a moat on their own; he often says a bigger brand could build the product anyway.
- **Going full-time:** expects founders with full energy and drive; he has said he will not work with founders whose energy does not match his.
- **Co-founders:** up to three is fine; he has said more than three invites conflicts of interest.
- **Debt:** comfortable mixing equity with loans, often at around 10 to 12 percent interest, as seen in deals like Namhya Foods, Stage, and Awayddings.
- **Dilution:** wary of heavy dilution; he points to boAt's founders keeping majority control.
- **Manufacturing in-house vs outsourced:** no recorded rule; boAt began by sourcing and branding products, and he leans on his manufacturing network when he says he could build it.
- **DTC vs retail:** online first, then offline. boAt grew online, and he earlier sold JBL through Indian retailers at Harman.
- **Subscription:** no recorded position.
- **Celebrity and influencer founders:** treats reach as a real asset; he praised a creator brand's fast sales on season 4.
- **Pre-revenue pitches:** usually cautious; wants proof that customers buy and come back.
- **Crowdfunding:** no recorded position.
- **Competing with Amazon:** sees marketplaces as a launch pad; boAt itself grew on them.
- **Competing with big brands:** a central question; he asks why a larger brand could not copy the product.
- **Consumer electronics and audio:** his home turf; he took 40 percent of Hammer, an audio and wearables brand, in season 1.
- **Fashion, food, and personal care:** frequent bets, such as Bummer, Let's Try, Skippi, and Snitch.
- **B2B, healthcare, and deep tech:** invests, usually with a partner Shark who knows the space, as with The Renal Project alongside Namita.
- **Pricing:** affordable and aspirational for the mass Indian market beats premium without proof.
- **Marketing spend:** frugal and return-driven; cash earned must balance cash burned.
- **Fundraising rejection:** funding is a founder's right; persist through rejection.
- **Burn rate:** watches it; boAt's own FY23 loss came from higher advertising and business costs.
- **When to say no:** no brand differentiation, founders who want to outsource marketing, premium pricing without proof, or terms he cannot honestly deliver.
- **Partnering with other Sharks:** a signature habit; most of his season 1 deals were joint.
- **Exits:** said in 2022 he wanted one or two exits from his angel portfolio and that some cash-generating businesses suit an individual investor even if they do not suit a VC fund. In 2025 he announced a large paper gain on Let's Try.
