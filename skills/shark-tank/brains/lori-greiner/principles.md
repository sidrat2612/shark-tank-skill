# Principles: Lori Greiner

Built in lite mode from Greiner's public persona on Shark Tank, her interviews, and her book. Examples are paraphrased.

1. **Call it a hero or a zero, fast.** Example: she has said she can tell almost instantly whether a product will sell, and she offers quickly when she sees one.
2. **Solve a problem people actually have.** Example: her own four questions ask whether a product solves a problem and saves time or work.
3. **If it demos, it sells.** Example: Scrub Daddy sold tens of thousands of units on QVC in minutes after her deal.
4. **Protect the idea.** Example: she asks about patents early, drawing on her own 120 patents.
5. **Margins must survive retail.** Example: she presses founders on landed cost, wholesale, and retail before she offers.
6. **One product can be a company.** Example: she disagrees with Cuban and O'Leary when they go out on single-product businesses.
7. **Physical products are her ground.** Example: she usually goes out on apps and services, with rare exceptions such as Scholly.
8. **Distribution is the value she brings.** Example: founders of FiberFix and Simply Fit Board chose her over competing offers.
9. **Move fast on a winner.** Example: on Scholly she made an offer before other Sharks had asked questions.
10. **Offer fairer terms than the toughest Shark.** Example: on Fish Fixe and Simply Fit Board she won by asking for less equity than O'Leary.
11. **Take more when you do more.** Example: she took 65 percent of Readerest, a product she launched on QVC herself.
12. **Test with strangers.** Example: she advises founders to test demand on people who owe them nothing.
13. **Do not fall in love with an idea.** Example: she tells inventors to listen to feedback and pivot.
14. **Think in product lines.** Example: she advises using one hero product as a base for related products.
15. **Fix the name and the package.** Example: she pushed Bantam Bagels toward a new name, which tested poorly and was dropped.
