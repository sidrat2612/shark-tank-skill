# Stories: Lori Greiner

Built in lite mode from public reporting on Greiner's career and her best-known Shark Tank deals, checked against the sources in sources.md. Figures appear only where widely reported.

1. **The earring organizer.** In 1996 she patented a plastic earring organizer, and JCPenney picked it up before the holidays. She paid off a 300,000 loan within 18 months. Lesson: one simple product that solves a common problem can build a company.
2. **For Your Ease Only.** She founded her company in 1996 and has since created more than 1,000 products and earned 120 patents. Lesson: protect what you invent, then keep inventing.
3. **Queen of QVC.** Her show, Clever and Unique Creations by Lori Greiner, has run on QVC since 2000. Lesson: live TV shopping can move huge volume for a product that demos well.
4. **Readerest.** In season 3, in 2012, Rick Hopper asked 150,000 for 15 percent for his magnetic eyeglass holder. She invested 150,000 for 65 percent, and it sold out on QVC within minutes. Lesson: she will take a big stake when she brings the whole launch.
5. **Scrub Daddy.** In October 2012 she invested 200,000 for 20 percent in Aaron Krause's sponge. They sold 42,000 sponges on QVC in under seven minutes, and it became one of the top-grossing products in the show's history. Lesson: a product that demos in seconds can become a hero.
6. **Drop Stop.** In 2013 she invested in the gap filler that stops items falling between car seats, choosing her offer over O'Leary's royalty offer. She later cited it as proof that a single product can be a big business. Lesson: an everyday annoyance plus a simple fix is her sweet spot.
7. **FiberFix.** In season 5 the founders asked 90,000 for 10 percent for their repair wrap. Herjavec offered the ask, but they took her offer of 120,000 for 12 percent. Lesson: founders often pick her for distribution, not just money.
8. **Rufflebutts.** In season 5 she offered 600,000 for 9 percent to the children's clothing company, and Cuban called it the worst deal she had ever offered. The deal did not close after the show. Lesson: a deal on air is not a deal done.
9. **Squatty Potty.** In November 2014, in season 6, she matched O'Leary's offer of 350,000 for 10 percent and won the deal. She helped get it into Bed Bath and Beyond. Lesson: an awkward, universal problem can sell big with the right retail push.
10. **Scholly.** In February 2015 she and Daymond John offered the founder of a scholarship app his full ask of 40,000 for 15 percent about ten minutes in. Herjavec, Cuban, and O'Leary objected that nobody had asked questions, and walked off. Lesson: her speed can feel like charity to the rest of the panel.
11. **Bantam Bagels.** In 2015 she offered 275,000 for 30 percent and accepted a counter at 25 percent. She pushed a rename that tested poorly. The company landed Starbucks, sold to T. Marzetti in 2018 for 34 million, and was shut down by 2022. Lesson: a strong launch does not guarantee a lasting product.
12. **Simply Fit Board.** In November 2015 a mother and daughter asked 125,000 for 15 percent. O'Leary offered double the money for 30 percent, and they chose her offer of 125,000 for 20 percent. Sales were reported at 160 million by 2021. Lesson: a fun, demo-able product can find a mass audience fast.
13. **Fish Fixe.** The founders asked 200,000 for 15 percent. O'Leary held firm at a third of the company, and she stepped in at 200,000 for 25 percent. Lesson: she wins deals by offering fairer terms than the toughest Shark.
14. **The single-product argument.** In a 2022 interview she said she disagrees with Cuban and O'Leary when they go out because a business is only one product, pointing to Scrub Daddy and Drop Stop. Lesson: one hero product can be a company, and a base for more.
