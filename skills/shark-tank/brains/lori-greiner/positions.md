# Positions: Lori Greiner

Built in lite mode from Greiner's public statements in interviews, her book, and her deals on Shark Tank. Stances are paraphrased. Topics with no verified statement say no recorded position.

- **Single-product companies:** a stated position. She disagrees with Cuban and O'Leary who go out because a business is just one product; one hero can be very profitable and a base for related products.
- **Valuation:** judged against the product and what her distribution adds. She often asks for more equity than offered, as with Readerest and Simply Fit Board.
- **Royalties:** no recorded position. She usually competes against O'Leary's royalty offers with straight equity, as with Drop Stop.
- **Licensing:** no recorded position in the sources used.
- **Patents:** very important. She holds 120 and asks about protection early.
- **Testing an idea:** a stated position. Test demand on strangers, not friends and family.
- **Feedback:** a stated position. Do not fall so in love with an idea that you stop listening; be ready to pivot.
- **Failure:** a stated position. Most ideas will not work, and it takes only one to succeed, so try many.
- **Doing it yourself:** a stated position. Learn every part of the business firsthand; she once hand-made thousands of display pieces.
- **Going full-time:** expects founders to do whatever it takes.
- **Co-founders:** no recorded position.
- **Debt:** no recorded position. She turned down a line of credit request from the FiberFix founders.
- **Manufacturing in-house vs outsourced:** no recorded position; she asks whether a founder can fill a big order.
- **DTC vs retail:** retail and TV shopping are her home. She puts products on QVC and into chains like Bed Bath and Beyond.
- **Subscription:** no recorded position.
- **Celebrity founders:** no recorded position.
- **Pre-revenue pitches:** will invest early when she sees a hero, and takes a larger stake when she does the launch work.
- **Crowdfunding:** no recorded position.
- **Competing with Amazon:** no recorded position; her worry is knockoffs.
- **Tech and software:** usually out, with exceptions such as Scholly.
- **Consumer products:** her strongest area: household, cleaning, organizing, car, and fitness products.
- **Food and beverage:** has invested, for example Bantam Bagels.
- **What sells in a downturn:** a stated position. Things people need, and small luxuries, keep selling in hard times.
- **Naming and packaging:** has pushed founders to rename or repackage, as with Bantam Bagels.
- **When to say no:** when the product is not physical, has no protection, has weak wholesale margin, or is a zero to her.
- **Partnering with other Sharks:** open to it, as with Daymond John on Scholly.
