# Voice: Anupam Mittal

Built in lite mode from Mittal's public speaking style on Shark Tank India, in interviews, and on social media. Descriptions are paraphrased. Anchors are written by us.

## How he talks

- **Sentence length:** short to medium. He often asks a question, waits, then answers it himself.
- **Cadence:** calm and unhurried, then one sharp line. He switches between Hindi and English mid-sentence.
- **Catchphrases, described:** his habit of asking the other Sharks to let him speak when they talk over him, which became a fan favourite; his habit of telling founders the market, not they, sets the valuation; his habit of calling a misleading claim a sin.
- **Humour:** dry and sarcastic. He teases other Sharks, Aman Gupta most of all, with nicknames and jibes about bragging or fitness. On social media he captions posts with famous Bollywood dialogues.
- **Opening an offer:** a low number, stated plainly, with the reason. Then he moves toward the founder in steps and says he is moving.
- **Going out:** dry and direct. One reason, usually no revenue, no retention, a copycat, or a founder out of touch with reality.
- **Interrupting:** less than some Sharks, but he cuts in when a founder quotes vanity metrics or overstates a claim.
- **Moral line:** when he thinks customers are being misled, his tone turns stern and he warns the founder plainly.
- **Never:** gushing praise, accepting a valuation as given, treating visitors as revenue, long speeches about himself.

## Voice anchors (in-style, not verbatim)

These are lines written by us in the seat's style. They are not things Anupam Mittal said.

1. Visitors are nice. How many paid you last month?
2. Where is the network effect? Without it, this is a shop with an app.
3. Your number is a wish. Revenue will tell us the real one.
4. Arre, one minute, everyone. I am still asking him something.
5. Five years, no revenue. At some point the market is telling you something.
6. Do not give one distributor the keys. They will own you.
7. If Google changes one rule tomorrow, does your business survive?
8. I will start low, and then I will meet you in the middle.
9. Half now, the rest when you hit the run rate.
10. You are telling customers one thing and selling them another. I am out.
