# Frameworks: Anupam Mittal

Built in lite mode from Mittal's companies, his reported interviews and posts, and his public behaviour on Shark Tank India. Paraphrased throughout.

## 1. Network effect test

- **What it is:** a platform is worth more when each new user makes it more useful for the others. Without that loop, it is a shop with an app.
- **Applied to a pitch:** ask whether more buyers attract more sellers, or more members make matches better, and look for proof in the numbers.
- **Scoring:** visible two-sided growth that feeds itself scores high. A one-sided business that buys every customer with ads scores low on this test.
- **Source:** Shaadi.com and Makaan.com, both two-sided platforms built inside People Group.

## 2. Retention before acquisition

- **What it is:** repeat use proves value. Signups, downloads, daily visitors, and followers do not.
- **Applied:** ask for month-two and month-six retention, repeat purchase rate, and revenue run rate before discussing growth spend.
- **Scoring:** cohorts that hold or improve score high. A founder with traffic but no paying, returning customers scores low.
- **Source:** Shark Tank India behaviour, for example his dismissal of a 3D interior design platform in season four that had users but no revenue after five years.

## 3. Valuation reset

- **What it is:** the founder does not set the valuation; the market does. Rebuild the number from revenue, growth, and traction.
- **Applied:** compute the implied valuation in crore, compare it to revenue, and open well below it.
- **Scoring:** not a score, a negotiation method. The gap between the ask and the rebuilt number decides how hard he pushes.
- **Source:** his stated view on valuation in season three; Shark Tank India behaviour across seasons.

## 4. Meet in the middle

- **What it is:** after a low opening, move toward the founder in visible steps to close a deal both sides can live with.
- **Applied:** counter the founder's counter, name the move, and stop at a number he can defend.
- **Scoring:** a structure rule, not a score.
- **Source:** Shark Tank India behaviour across seasons.

## 5. Milestone tranche

- **What it is:** split the cheque. Part now, part when the business hits a stated revenue target.
- **Applied:** offer a first tranche at his valuation and a second tranche tied to a run-rate target, with equity that can flex on performance.
- **Scoring:** a structure rule. It lets him back an early founder without paying today for tomorrow's numbers.
- **Source:** his season four deal with Repeat Gud, where a second tranche was tied to a monthly run-rate target.

## 6. Copycat wedge check

- **What it is:** a copy of a funded leader needs a specific wedge: a segment, a geography, a price point, or a trust edge the leader ignores.
- **Applied:** name the funded leader and ask what this does that the leader cannot or will not.
- **Scoring:** a sharp, defensible wedge scores well. Me too with less money is near fatal.
- **Source:** Shaadi.com competing for years with other matrimony platforms; Shark Tank India behaviour.

## 7. Platform and gatekeeper risk

- **What it is:** a business that depends on one app store, search engine, marketplace, or distributor can be squeezed or switched off.
- **Applied:** ask what share of sales flows through one channel and what happens if that channel changes terms. Warn founders against exclusive deals that hand a distributor control.
- **Scoring:** owned or diversified channels score high. Total dependence on one gatekeeper scores low.
- **Source:** his public fight with Google over Play Store billing and the 2024 delisting of Shaadi.com; his warning about distributor power on Shark Tank India in 2025.

## 8. Trust as the product

- **What it is:** in a marketplace, and in any health or wellness claim, users pay for trust. Misleading the customer ends the conversation.
- **Applied:** ask how the business verifies supply, backs its claims, and handles bad actors.
- **Scoring:** a clear trust system scores well. A misleading claim is an automatic out.
- **Source:** Shaadi.com; his season two rejection of a diet brand he saw as misleading customers.

## 9. Full-time founder test

- **What it is:** a startup needs the founder's constant presence. A part-time founder, however famous, loses to one who is all in.
- **Applied:** ask whether the founder is full-time and what else competes for their attention.
- **Scoring:** a full-time, hands-on founder is the baseline. A celebrity or influencer running it on the side scores low.
- **Source:** his season four challenge to a YouTuber's protein brand.

## 10. Market size sanity

- **What it is:** the reachable online market must be large enough for a big outcome to justify the risk.
- **Applied:** ask how many customers can actually be reached online and at what spend.
- **Scoring:** a clear path to a large reachable market scores high. A niche with a ceiling scores low for him, even if profitable.
- **Source:** People Group's businesses across matrimony, property, and mobile; Shark Tank India behaviour.
