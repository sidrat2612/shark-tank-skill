# Stories: Anupam Mittal

Built in lite mode from widely reported facts about Mittal's companies, his angel investing, and his Shark Tank India deals and moments. Deal figures appear only where widely reported, and are the on-air terms; deals can change after the show.

1. **Boom and bust.** Working at MicroStrategy in the US, he became a paper multi-millionaire in his twenties, then lost it in the dotcom crash and returned to India in 2003 with little left. Lesson: paper wealth is not a business.
2. **Shaadi.com.** He started Sagai.com in 1997 under People Group, renamed it Shaadi.com, and put most of his remaining money into it after returning. It grew into one of India's leading matrimony platforms. Lesson: a two-sided platform gets stronger with every member.
3. **Raising money with no local VCs.** He has said India had few venture investors then, pitched widely in the US, and closed Shaadi.com's first institutional round in 2006 with Sequoia, Silicon Valley Bank, and Westbridge. Lesson: persistence closes rounds.
4. **Makaan.com.** People Group's property marketplace was acquired by the News Corp backed PropTiger in 2015. Lesson: build a listings platform others want to own.
5. **Angel investing.** His first angel cheque was in Interactive Avenues in 2007. He was one of Ola's earliest investors and has backed over 250 startups, including BigBasket and Rapido. Lesson: back two-sided networks early.
6. **Fighting Google.** In 2024 Google pulled Shaadi.com and other Indian apps from the Play Store over billing rules, and he called it a dark day for Indian internet. Lesson: platform risk is real even for market leaders.
7. **Season one total.** He committed about 5.4 crore rupees to 24 businesses in Shark Tank India season one. Lesson: a wide portfolio of small bets.
8. **Skippi Ice Pops.** Asked for 45 lakh for 5 percent and got 1 crore for 15 percent from all five Sharks in season one. He later named it among his top Shark Tank investments. Lesson: more money for more equity can suit both sides.
9. **Sunfox Technologies.** The Spandan pocket ECG maker asked for 1 crore for 2 percent and closed an all-shark deal at 1 crore for 6 percent in season one. Lesson: hard tech with a big market can win the whole panel.
10. **Revamp Moto.** He and Aman Gupta won the electric vehicle startup at 1 crore for 1.5 percent, against a rival bid from Ashneer Grover, in season one. Lesson: compete hard for deals that can scale.
11. **Thinkerbell Labs.** The maker of the Annie braille learning device got 1.05 crore for 3 percent from him, Namita Thapar, and Peyush Bansal in season one. Lesson: a defensible product with a clear mission.
12. **CosIQ.** The skincare brand asked for 50 lakh for 7.5 percent and closed at 50 lakh for 25 percent with him and Vineeta Singh in season one. Lesson: a valuation reset at its hardest.
13. **Zillionaire.** Two 19-year-old founders of a Jaipur jewellery brand asked for 50 lakh for 3.3 percent; he alone gave them 1 crore for 10 percent in season two. Lesson: pay above the ask when you want a bigger stake.
14. **Repeat Gud.** In season four he offered 50 lakh for 10 percent in the vegan sauce brand, with a second tranche tied to a revenue run-rate target. Lesson: tie later money to milestones.
15. **The FOMO admission.** After season one he said he had made some deals out of fear of missing out in the heat of the show. Lesson: competition between Sharks can override judgement.
