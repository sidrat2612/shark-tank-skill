# Frameworks: Kevin O'Leary

Built in lite mode from O'Leary's Cold Hard Truth books, his fund work, interviews, and his public behaviour on Shark Tank. Paraphrased throughout.

## 1. Royalty payback rule

- **What it is:** the investor is repaid from sales, not from a future exit. A fixed amount per unit, or a percentage of sales, until the capital is back, then a smaller royalty for good.
- **Applied to a pitch:** price the royalty against unit margin and sales volume, then estimate how many months until payback.
- **Scoring:** payback inside roughly two to three years on current sales scores well. Payback that depends on projected sales scores low.
- **Source:** Shark Tank deals such as Wicked Good Cupcakes and Roq Innovation; Dragons' Den.

## 2. Valuation reality check

- **What it is:** the ask divided by the equity gives the valuation. That number has to make sense against sales and profit.
- **Applied:** state the implied valuation out loud, then compare it to trailing sales and to what a buyer would pay.
- **Scoring:** a valuation near a few times sales, with profit, is sane. A valuation many times sales with no profit is crazy and usually means out.
- **Source:** Shark Tank behaviour across seasons.

## 3. Money as soldiers

- **What it is:** every dollar is a soldier sent out to war each day, expected to come home with prisoners. Capital that does not come back is lost in battle.
- **Applied:** ask what each dollar of the investment will do and how it returns.
- **Scoring:** a specific use of funds tied to more sales scores well. Money for salaries or vague marketing scores low.
- **Source:** repeated on Shark Tank, in interviews, and on his social media.

## 4. Recurring revenue premium

- **What it is:** revenue that repeats on its own is worth more than one-time sales.
- **Applied:** ask about subscriptions, refills, repeat rate, and churn.
- **Scoring:** high repeat with low churn lifts the valuation he will accept. One-time sales with no reorder pull it down.
- **Source:** Shark Tank behaviour.

## 5. Dividend discipline

- **What it is:** a good business pays its owners cash from operations. A company that never pays out has to prove why.
- **Applied:** ask when the business can distribute cash, and whether it can fund its own growth.
- **Scoring:** a business that can pay out within a few years scores high. One that needs funding round after round scores low.
- **Source:** O'Leary Funds, O'Shares ETFs, and his public investing commentary.

## 6. Know your numbers

- **What it is:** a founder must state sales, gross margin, unit cost, customer cost, break-even timing, and market size without notes.
- **Applied:** fire the numbers questions in order and watch for guessing.
- **Scoring:** clean answers are the baseline. A founder who does not know margin is close to out.
- **Source:** his CNBC interviews on pitching and Shark Tank behaviour.

## 7. Margin first

- **What it is:** gross margin decides whether growth makes money or burns it.
- **Applied:** compare unit cost to price, then subtract retail and shipping costs.
- **Scoring:** healthy margin (roughly 40 percent or more for a physical product) scores well. Thin margin with big growth plans scores low.
- **Source:** Shark Tank behaviour.

## 8. Venture debt structure

- **What it is:** a loan with interest, sometimes with a small equity kicker, for a business with steady cash flow.
- **Applied:** offered when the business is real and profitable but the valuation is too high for equity.
- **Scoring:** not a score, a structure rule. It fits businesses with predictable cash, not pre-revenue ideas.
- **Source:** Shark Tank behaviour.

## 9. Kill the dead deal

- **What it is:** once the math cannot work, stop. Time spent on a dead deal is money lost.
- **Applied:** say the deal is dead and give the one reason, usually valuation or no path to repayment.
- **Scoring:** not a score, an exit rule.
- **Source:** Shark Tank behaviour across seasons.
