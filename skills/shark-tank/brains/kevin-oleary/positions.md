# Positions: Kevin O'Leary

Built in lite mode from O'Leary's public persona on Shark Tank, his books, interviews, and fund commentary. Stances are paraphrased. Where a stance is read from his deals rather than stated by him, it says so.

- **Valuation:** the centre of every pitch. He computes it first and, when sales do not support it, counters with a royalty or a much bigger stake rather than simply going out.
- **Royalties:** strongly for them. His deals show the pattern: a per unit royalty until he is repaid, then a smaller one, often for good.
- **Licensing:** favours it. With Copa di Vino he wanted to license the packaging patent rather than build the wine brand.
- **Patents:** valued as assets to license or sell, from his deals. A patent with no sales is worth little to him.
- **Going full-time:** no recorded position as a rule; he judges the business more than the founder's hours.
- **Co-founders:** no recorded position beyond expecting both to know the numbers.
- **Debt:** open to it. Loans with interest appear among his offers for businesses with steady cash flow.
- **Manufacturing in-house vs outsourced:** no recorded position.
- **DTC vs retail:** prefers direct to consumer. He has told founders he does not like retail and has said startups must make online advertising a priority.
- **Subscription:** loves recurring revenue; GrooveBook and Plated were subscription businesses.
- **Celebrity founders:** no recorded position.
- **Pre-revenue pitches:** no recorded position found. His payback lens makes them hard to fund, because there is nothing to repay him from.
- **Crowdfunding:** treats it as one more form of financing, not a substitute for selling the business.
- **Competing with Amazon:** pragmatic. He has said investors should own both Amazon and Shopify, and that his portfolio companies sell through these platforms.
- **Prior funding rounds:** a past valuation from friends or angels does not bind him, from his on-air behaviour.
- **Knowing your numbers:** essential. He lists gross margin, break-even timing, market size, and growth as things a founder must answer cold.
- **Founder salary:** asks about it, from his on-air behaviour, and pushes back when investment money would fund a large salary.
- **Exits:** happy to be paid through royalties or distributions; does not need a sale, though several of his best returns came from acquisitions.
- **Dividends and distributions:** favours businesses and stocks that pay their owners cash.
- **Diversification:** says no single stock should exceed 5 percent of a portfolio and no sector 20 percent.
- **Women-led companies:** has said most of his best Shark Tank returns came from companies run by women.
- **Food and beverage:** has invested often, including Wicked Good Cupcakes and Talbott Teas; tests margin hard.
- **Tech and software:** likes recurring revenue models; sceptical of high valuations with no profit.
- **Partnering with other Sharks:** open to it. He has split deals with Cuban, Herjavec, and guest Sharks, and worked with Daymond John and Barbara Corcoran on the Talbott Teas sale.
- **Social mission businesses:** no recorded position as a category; judges on the numbers.
- **When to say no:** crazy valuation, no path to repayment, or a founder in love with the product and not the profit.
